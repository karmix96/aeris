# S7 research basis

This note separates capabilities documented by primary/official sources from
evidence measured on AERIS. Documentation can justify a candidate method; it cannot
qualify the thin trailing edge, tip, prism topology, y+, or force accuracy.

## Gmsh and the provisional hybrid topology

The [Gmsh reference manual](https://gmsh.info/doc/texinfo/) documents discrete
entities, physical groups, mesh-size fields, 3-D algorithms, and
`extrudeBoundaryLayer`. The original
[Gmsh paper](https://orbi.uliege.be/handle/2268/22742) describes the open-source
architecture and meshing algorithms. The HXT work covers
[parallel Delaunay tetrahedralization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08831) and
[quality tetrahedral mesh generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08508).

These sources support testing a fixed triangular wall, normal prism extrusion, and
Delaunay/HXT tet core. They do not show that the simple extrusion is robust at this
BWB's thin numerical TE or closed tip. Gmsh's 3-D boundary-layer extrusion has no
automatic fan/re-entrant-corner treatment equivalent to a dedicated aerospace
advancing-layer method, so TE/tip prism coverage, column continuity, adjacent-core
quality, and inversion are explicit hard gates.

S7 imports an independently sampled pyGeo wall as an immutable discrete triangular
surface. Consequently, `Mesh.Algorithm` in the retry sequence applies only to
surfaces generated by Gmsh around that wall, principally the farfield. It is not a
hidden wall-remeshing retry. The scientifically meaningful core alternatives are
Delaunay and HXT; all candidates retain identical wall geometry, physical sizes,
and prism schedules.

Synthetic AERIS diagnostics found that Gmsh `Relocate3D` changes intermediate prism
nodes after extrusion. A nominally core-scoped relocation also changed the layer
schedule. Post-generation optimization is therefore frozen off before any real BWB
development result. This is local synthetic evidence about that operation, not a
finding that the unoptimized BWB path is robust.

## Geometry and mesh-quality instrumentation

The common study brief records multiple verifier bugs, including fidelity checks
that sampled the wrong geometric object or silently skipped refined blocks. S7
therefore uses several independent checks:

- every tracked OML vertex is re-evaluated at its exact pyGeo `(u,v)` coordinate;
- every upper/lower triangle centroid is compared with the exact opened B-spline
  at the corresponding parametric centroid, bounding planar-facet error rather
  than merely proving vertices lie on the source;
- Gmsh wall nodes are matched geometrically after any node renumbering;
- native SU2 points and boundary/volume topology are reconstructed and compared
  with Gmsh rather than trusting the writer's counts;
- source, written surface, volume, prism columns, and TE/tip interfaces have
  separate fail-closed gates.

[Knupp's algebraic quality framework](https://doi.org/10.1137/S1064827500371499)
explains why Jacobian/condition metrics are useful but not interchangeable. The
[Sandia Verdict library report](https://www.sandia.gov/research/publications/details/the-verdict-geometric-quality-library-2006-03-01/)
provides an independent vocabulary for element quality. S7 therefore does not use
one scalar as a universal proxy. It separately gates signed volumes, Gmsh tet
`minSICN`, prism `minSJ`, minimum determinants, aspect ratio, equiangle skewness,
face non-orthogonality, adjacent-volume ratios, layer geometry, and regional
TE/tip values. High prism aspect ratio is reported and bounded separately rather
than misclassified through a tet-oriented condition metric.

## SU2 RANS-SA, output, convergence, and restart

SU2 is fixed; a mesher problem is not an excuse to change the solver. The
[SU2 paper](https://su2code.github.io/documents/SU2_AIAA_ASM2013.pdf) is the primary
method reference. Official documentation defines the
[native mesh format](https://su2code.github.io/docs/Mesh-File/),
[custom history/surface output](https://su2code.github.io/docs_v7/Custom-Output/),
and [restart files](https://su2code.github.io/docs/Restart-File/). The
[official SU2 configuration template](https://github.com/su2code/SU2/blob/master/config_template.cfg)
is the option-name authority.

S7 writes native SU2 elements itself and independently reconstructs every tet/prism
face. Every boundary face must belong to exactly one required marker, and no marker
face may be internal. SU2 output is requested for all four wall markers so y+ can be
selected by exact mesh point IDs even when the CSV does not carry a marker column.
History acceptance requires a finite density-residual trace, six-order drop,
absolute final threshold, and stable finite CL/CD/CMy tails. A zero process exit is
not convergence. At most one restart is permitted, and its input digest must equal
the immediately preceding output.

The current machine lacks `SU2_CFD`, so option compatibility and the real restart
path remain unverified. The fake-solver fixture proves parser, preservation, and
control-flow behavior only.

## Wall resolution and discretization evidence

NASA's [Turbulence Modeling Resource](https://turbmodels.larc.nasa.gov/) provides
low-Reynolds-number RANS verification cases and grid families. Its
[SA verification material](https://turbmodels.larc.nasa.gov/naca0012numerics_val_sa_withpv.html)
shows why the trailing edge and grid definition affect a nominally simple case.
NASA's [low-Re grid/y+ discussion](https://turbmodels.larc.nasa.gov/Papers/aiaa2008-4403lowRe.pdf)
supports treating wall y+ as measured solution evidence, not something inferred
from nominal first height. The
[nested-grid examples](https://turbmodels.larc.nasa.gov/hc_grids.html) motivate
controlled refinement.

S7 consequently refines surface, prism, wake, near-core, and far-core resolution
together. Qualification uses actual cell counts, `h=N_volume^(-1/3)`, the unequal-
ratio Richardson equation, and GCI with explicit non-asymptotic and maximum-fine-GCI
failures. The 0.5, 1.0, and 1.5 mm numerical-TE variants are compared only at the
same design, flow, grid level, and force normalization. Literature thresholds or a
coarse y+ result are never promoted to BWB production proof.

## Alternative open-source meshers

The official [snappyHexMesh documentation](https://doc.openfoam.com/2606/tools/pre-processing/mesh/generation/snappyhexmesh/)
describes castellated/snapped volume meshing, layer addition, and mesh-quality
controls. [cfMesh](https://cfmesh.com/cfmesh/) is another automated OpenFOAM-family
route. [Netgen](https://github.com/NGSolve/netgen) and
[TetGen](https://tetgen.github.io/) are credible tetrahedral-core candidates.

None is a drop-in recovery setting:

- snappyHexMesh/cfMesh change the core topology, layer algorithm, dependencies,
  quality vocabulary, and SU2 conversion path;
- Netgen/TetGen address tetrahedralization but do not by themselves replace the
  audited near-wall prism construction;
- changing any candidate after seeing development outcomes introduces a new
  experimental method.

Gmsh therefore remains the provisional baseline. If all frozen candidates share
the same BWB TE/tip topology or quality failure, preserve every attempt, stop S7,
and preregister one alternative in a superseding ADR. A threshold is never weakened
and another mesher is never substituted silently.

## Evidence boundary

At this revision there is no real S7 BWB mesh or CFD evidence. Primary sources,
code, passing tests, synthetic fixtures, plans, and failed execution approvals are
not campaign results. A result is usable only with its evidence tier, current
source/policy/software identities, immutable terminal manifests, verified hashes,
and all applicable technical and governance gates.


## Automating unstructured meshing for a fixed-topology design family (2026-08-22)

### Why sliver tetrahedra survive next to the prism cap

Slivers are the characteristic failure of 3-D Delaunay tetrahedralisation: four
vertices near the equator of their circumsphere, almost zero volume, and a small
radius-edge ratio, so the standard radius-edge quality criterion cannot even see
them ([Labelle, Sliver Removal by Lattice Refinement](http://wismuth.com/papers/sliver.pdf);
[Tournois et al., Perturbing Slivers in 3D Delaunay Meshes](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/40737375_Perturbing_Slivers_in_3D_Delaunay_Meshes)).
The literature reports sliver-free results with dihedral angles bounded away from
degeneracy **except near the boundary**, and that exception is precisely S7's
situation: the prism cap is a hard constraint the core optimiser may not move, so
a sliver touching it cannot be perturbed, exuded or swapped away.

This matches what was measured here.  Netgen scoped to the core removes interior
slivers readily (index 0 tet SICN minimum 0.029 to 0.146) but plateaus against
the cap: index 49 improves 0.0404, 0.0430, 0.0443 for one, two and three passes
and does not reach the 0.05 limit.  Only two cells of 918 670 are involved, both
0.038 L from the wall, immediately above a 0.0287 L prism stack.  A recognised
sliver-removal method is not missing; the elements are boundary-locked.

Independent practitioner reports of Gmsh hybrid meshing describe the same
behaviour, noting that the Delaunay algorithm tends to produce ill-shaped
elements where a manually meshed region meets the unstructured fill, and
recommending the mesh-quality plugin to locate them
([Gmsh hybrid meshing discussion](https://www.cfd-online.com/Forums/openfoam-meshing/161688-gmsh-2d-airfoil-boundary-layer-hybrid-mesh.html);
[NASA TM, Methods for Prismatic/Tetrahedral Grid Generation and Adaptation](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19960011648/downloads/19960011648.pdf)).
The prism-for-boundary-layer, tetrahedra-for-freestream split S7 uses, built by
normal extrusion with a prescribed growth to a y+ target, is the standard
construction rather than an unusual one.

### Distribution quality is resolution dependent, and that is not a defect

Binary correctness - closure, manifoldness, orientation, labels, prism coverage
and continuity, conversion fidelity, cell validity - is resolution independent.
Face-quality *distributions* are not.  Measured on index 0 with everything else
fixed: skewness p99 of 0.890, 0.872, 0.873 and 0.737 at 77 348, 136 167, 195 599
and 1 207 177 cells, with non-orthogonality p99 of 75.3, 73.2, 73.5 and 57.7.

A structured hexahedral mesh does not behave this way, which is why S6 could
apply a single quality floor at smoke resolution and still pass 100 of 100.  For
S7 the same policy would measure the tier rather than the method, so the
distribution gates are enforced from the development and production tiers and
reported below them.  The broad 100-design gate therefore tests what S6's smoke
atlas actually tested: that meshing succeeds across the design space without
manual repair.

### Exploiting the fixed topology: morph rather than remesh

Every AERIS BWB shares one parameterisation, so the design family has fixed
topology.  The established way to exploit that is radial-basis-function mesh
morphing: mesh once and deform the existing volume mesh to each new shape,
preserving topology, avoiding remeshing noise, and costing a small fraction of a
solve ([Biancolini et al., RBF morphing techniques for simulation-based design
optimization](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00366-013-0330-1);
[Automatic parametrization and mesh deformation for CFD optimization](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.6190)).

S6 already works this way - its atlas deforms a template onto a target, measured
at 1 621 504 cells with zero inverted cells - so the S7 analogue is direct and is
the strongest available answer to generalisation:

1. Freeze the surface sampling counts across the family so every design shares
   one connectivity and node correspondence is exact and index-based, which is
   stronger than the geometric correspondence generic RBF morphing must infer.
2. Mesh a small number of template designs to full quality once.
3. Deform a template's volume mesh onto each remaining design and audit the
   result with the existing instruments, falling back to a full remesh when the
   deformed mesh fails.

The prerequisite is step 1: surface triangle counts currently vary per design
(1 778, 1 646, 1 646, 1 778, 1 778 across the representative indices) because the
sampling counts are derived from each design's own chord and span.
