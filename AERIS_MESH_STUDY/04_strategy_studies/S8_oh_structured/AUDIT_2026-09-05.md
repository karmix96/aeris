# S8 O-H structured grid — audit for external review

**Date:** 2026-09-05 · **Geometry:** `lhs100_seed42[83]`, a blended-wing-body UAV
half model · **Solver:** ADflow RANS, Spalart–Allmaras · **Operating point:**
M 0.0837, Re 1.53e6, 28 m/s at 1500 m

This document is written to be audited. Every number in it is measured and
traceable to a committed report; where something is an inference rather than a
measurement it says so. The question it exists to answer is at the end:
**is this ready to spend desktop time on?**

---

## 1. What S8 is, and the problem it was built to solve

S8 is the fourth meshing strategy in a governed tournament (ADR-0011). Its
predecessors are relevant because they define the gates:

- **S2** (cross-field quad layout, after Yu et al. 2026) produced excellent
  meshes on one geometry, then block counts of 27/31/37/39 across six designs.
  Rejected on **topology determinism**, not on quality.
- **S6/S7** (the "C family", six blocks per section with leading- and
  trailing-edge collars) reached CFD but carried a defect: the nose collar's
  width is a block dimension, `end_points`, shared with the trailing-edge base
  block. Leading-edge resolution was starved at every level — 2, 3 and 4 wrap
  cells absorbing **37.9, 31.8 and 25.7 degrees of surface turning per cell**
  against a conventional wall-resolved reference of 10. On candidate C03 this
  drove surface cp above its physical bound on **137 of 296** leading-edge
  collar faces, peaking at **cp 5.328**, and that strip supplied roughly 80 % of
  pressure drag. A census found the same construction in all 65 surface meshes
  in the study; zero passed.
- The attempt to fix it inside that topology (the "D family") reached
  10.6 deg/cell and then **would not march**: `candidate_d03` segmentation-faulted
  at every governed setting, 25 marches in total, because `end_points` also sets
  the point count across the 1.0 mm blunt trailing edge and `end_scale` also
  contracts the tip cap.

**The coupling, not the parameter values, was the defect.** S8 removes it
structurally.

### Topology

```
xi    one closed O-ring per section: upper TE corner -> LE -> lower TE corner
      -> across the blunt base.  ONE block, no collars.
eta   wall-normal, marched in the section plane to a circular far field.
zeta  spanwise, root symmetry plane -> tip -> spanwise far field.
```

Three volume blocks: `o_wing`, `o_out`, `cap_out`. **Two surface blocks** for the
whole closed wing, against thirteen for candidate C03.

Leading-edge resolution is requested in **degrees of turning per cell** and the
spacing is *solved for* on the ring that was actually built. There is no
`end_points`, so nothing the leading edge asks for can shrink the trailing edge
or the tip cap.

## 2. Construction, step by step

1. **Geometry.** pyGeo loft from the canonical config, via
   `strategy_s6.build_pygeo_case`. 29 extracted sections, semispan 0.978 m,
   twist +0.225 deg at root to −6.039 deg at tip (washout).
2. **Section ring.** Two-sided hyperbolic (Vinokur) stretching around the closed
   section, solved by bisection so the first and last intervals are the requested
   trailing-edge and leading-edge spacings. Nodes are re-evaluated on the exact
   loft at the solved parameters, not interpolated from a polyline.
3. **Leading-edge spacing.** Bisection on the delivered turning, measured on the
   built ring. A closed-form `ds = R·theta` was tried and rejected: a three-point
   circle fit across the nose overestimates R threefold, so asking for 6 degrees
   that way delivered 20.
4. **Spanwise smoothing of the LE spacing.** A minimum envelope, ≤1.15 growth per
   station, applied after the per-station solve. See defect 15.
5. **Span law.** Two-sided stretching solved in spanwise arc length and inverted
   onto the loft parameter, so the last cell is a requested number rather than a
   consequence. This is what sets tip-cap y+.
6. **Wall-normal march.** Algebraic, in the section plane, blending from the wall
   normal to radial with Laplacian smoothing that grows with distance from the
   wall. **Deliberately not pyHyp**: pyHyp's march is what segmentation-faulted
   25 times on the D family, and its failure mode is a crash with no diagnosis.
   An algebraic march cannot crash, and its cells can be checked afterwards.
7. **Far-field frame blend.** Each section's marching plane rotates from its own
   best-fit plane at the wall to a common orientation at the far field, so the
   outer boundary is a cylinder of parallel circles that cannot intersect.
8. **Tip closure.** One chordwise transfinite H patch whose perimeter *is* the
   tip ring, node for node, with **zero collapsed edges**. A butterfly cap was
   tried first and rejected on measurement: the tip ring's distance to its own
   centroid ranges 7.7 to 80.2 mm, so a 65 % pull moved points up to 52 mm
   across a section only 22.9 mm thick, and upper and lower surfaces crossed.
9. **Volume validity.** Signed hex volumes over the assembled block, climbing a
   smoothing ladder until zero are negative; the build **raises** if it cannot.

## 4. CFD results

Four angles on `oh_L3`, 6 MPI ranks, ~30 min each.

| alpha | CL | CD | CDp | CDv | CMy | rel. residual | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| −2 | −0.298397 | 0.026005 | 0.017417 | 0.008588 | −0.005953 | 9.96e-09 | ACCEPTED |
| 0 | −0.159963 | 0.020838 | 0.011841 | 0.008996 | +0.001280 | 9.14e-09 | ACCEPTED |
| 4 | +0.120266 | 0.019864 | 0.010440 | 0.009425 | +0.015378 | 5.73e-08 | ACCEPTED_SOLVER_FROZEN |
| 8 | +0.401834 | 0.030922 | 0.021511 | 0.009411 | +0.028522 | 8.76e-09 | ACCEPTED |

**Reference contract, verified identical on all four runs from their own
`result.json`:** `areaRef` 0.394918242017589 m² (half model), moment reference
(0.4, 0, 0) m, `chordRef` 0.9 m.

### Lift curve

Slope per interval: **0.06922, 0.07006, 0.07039 per degree** — linear to 1.7 %,
gently increasing, which is what a real wing does at low to moderate incidence.

Negative CL at alpha 0 is physical, not a defect: the geometry carries washout
(twist −6.039 deg at the tip), so at zero incidence the outer wing flies at
negative effective incidence.

### Convergence gate (adopted 2026-09-05, replacing a bare 1e-8 residual cutoff)

Residual ≥ 5 orders **and** CL stable < 0.05 % **and** CD < 0.1 % **and**
|ΔCMy| < 1e-4 over 100 iterations **and** no divergence or growing oscillation.

Two design points worth auditing:

- **The CMy tolerance is absolute, not relative**, because CMy crosses zero
  between alpha −2 and 0 and a relative tolerance there demands infinite
  precision at the crossing.
- **A frozen residual is reported separately from a converged one.** "The forces
  stopped moving" has two causes — the solution converged, or the solver stopped
  stepping — and a gate reading only force flatness cannot tell them apart.
  Alpha 4's forces are stable to 9.2e-09 (five orders inside the limit) precisely
  because *nothing is happening*.

### The alpha 4 stall — unresolved

Residual frozen at **5.2371905e-05 to eight significant figures** over 150+
iterations; 7.242 of the 8 orders required. NK **step length 0.00** and **linear
residual 1.000** — the Krylov solve stopped reducing, so Newton–Krylov takes null
steps. Not a mesh-validity problem: zero inverted cells, forces settled.

It is **not** the lift-axis defect (see defect 14): alpha 4 carries no sideslip.
Untested candidates: the tip cap's one cell at scaled Jacobian 0.049; a cell
volume span of ~1e15 across the domain (3.6e-14 to 1.5e+02 m³).

Its flow field no longer exists — the run was stopped to unblock alpha 8, and
ADflow writes solutions only at the end.

### Surface pressure against its physical bound (isentropic cp ≤ 1.0018)

| alpha | over bound | TE corner | leading edge | peak excess | share of CDp |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| −2 | 14 | 11 | 3 | 0.0911 | 1.76 % |
| 0 | 23 | 20 | 3 | 0.1809 | 2.66 % |
| 8 | 61 | 25 | **36** | **0.4626** | 3.15 % |

Against C03's **137 of 296** faces peaking at **cp 5.328**.

Clipping every excess to the bound moves CDp by **−0.096 %, −0.212 %, −0.268 %**.
The defect is real and small.

Two distinct causes:

- **Trailing-edge corner** cells absorb **86–106 degrees of turning in one cell**
  — four times worse per cell than the C-family collar S8 exists to replace. It
  is a **C0 kink**, so refinement cannot reduce it, and "degrees of turning per
  cell" is the wrong instrument there. Aerodynamically negligible: those cells'
  normals are nearly streamwise, and they carry 0.01–0.2 % of CDp.
- **Leading edge**: 3 cells at cruise, 36 at alpha 8. `target_le_turn_deg 10` was
  calibrated at cruise attitude; the high-load case sharpens the suction peak.
  **Leading-edge resolution is operating-point dependent, and the level
  definitions do not express that.**

## 5. Independent cross-validation against AVL

AVL run on the **identical pyGeo loft** — the same `build_pygeo_case` call the
mesher makes — so both codes see one loft, not two models of it.

**Reference areas match**: AVL `s_ref` 0.78984 m² against the CFD full area
0.789836 m², so CL is directly comparable. AVL was rerun taking moments about
(0.4, 0, 0), the CFD's own point, so no arm is applied by hand.

| alpha | CFD CL | AVL CL | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| −2 | −0.29840 | −0.33038 | +0.03198 |
| 0 | −0.15996 | −0.18575 | +0.02579 |
| 4 | +0.12027 | +0.10491 | +0.01536 |
| 8 | +0.40183 | +0.39435 | **+0.00748** |

The gap **closes monotonically with incidence**, to 1.9 % at alpha 8. That is the
right direction: at alpha 0 the flow is set by camber and viscous decambering,
where an inviscid method differs most; at higher alpha it is set by incidence,
where a vortex-lattice method is at its best. AVL's slope is 0.0723–0.0727 per
degree (linear by construction) against the CFD's 0.0692–0.0704.

### Static stability

| | dCMy/dCL | neutral point |
|---|---|---|
| CFD, from ADflow's CMy | +0.0492 | 0.3557 m |
| CFD, raw pressure integration | — | **0.3561 m** |
| AVL, from its four points | +0.0698 | 0.3372 m |
| AVL, its own reported `x_np` | — | 0.3359 m |

**Both codes agree the aircraft is statically UNSTABLE about x = 0.4 m**, and
their neutral points differ by **3.7 % of MAC**. The centre of pressure moves
forward with lift (0.3818 m at alpha −2 to 0.3367 m at alpha 8), which is
instability read directly off the pressure field with no sign convention
involved.

**Design consequence:** if 0.4 m is the intended CG, index 83 is unstable in
pitch by roughly 9 % of MAC. That is a design input, and a statement about index
83 alone.

## 6. The grid family ("the ladder")

### The original ladder was not a convergence family

`oh_L3 / oh_L2 / oh_L1` refined the three directions at **1.31 / 1.33 / 1.25**
and simultaneously moved the leading-edge target 10 → 8 → 6 degrees and the
trailing-edge fraction 0.004 → 0.003. With the resolution *law* changing between
levels, a shift in CD cannot be attributed to the grid, and Richardson
extrapolation over it would produce a number that means nothing. Effective
ratios were 1.2954 and **1.3523** — the second outside the declared 1.35 ceiling.

### The replacement is generated from one ratio

`refined_level(base, r)` scales intervals in all three directions by r and holds
fixed everything that decides *where* cells go. `gci_C` **is** `oh_L3` bit for
bit, so the four-angle sweep above is the family's coarse level.

| level | cells | inverted | LE turning | s0 (m) | memory floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gci_C` | 567,256 | 0 | 10.019 deg | 4.72e-6 | 5.62 GiB — **solved** |
| `gci_M` | 1,111,152 | 0 | 7.202 deg | 3.63e-6 | 11.00 GiB |
| `gci_F` | 2,217,680 | 0 | 5.073 deg | 2.80e-6 | 21.96 GiB |
| `gci_FF` | 4,504,420 | 0 | 3.502 deg | 2.14e-6 | 44.60 GiB |

**Delivered spacing ratios, measured mid-span, against a nominal 1.300:**

```
gci_C -> gci_M    LE 1.310   TE 1.300   s0 1.298
gci_M -> gci_F    LE 1.286   TE 1.308   s0 1.299
```

All within 1.5 %. Leading-edge turning now falls as an **outcome** rather than
being dialled in, which is what makes the cp-bound question answerable: if the 36
over-bound LE cells at alpha 8 fall with refinement, that is resolution.

### Getting the leading edge to refine took three failed attempts, all measured

| approach | delivered LE refinement |
|---|---|
| hold `target_le_turn_deg` fixed | 0.881, 0.963, 1.009 — *coarser* |
| scale the target by 1/r | 1.142, 1.121 |
| divide each level's own solved `ds_le` | 1.056, 1.080 |
| **solve at the baseline `n_side`, then divide** | **1.310, 1.286** |

The second fails because `ds = R·theta` needs constant R and a shrinking cell
sits nearer the nose where curvature is higher. The third fails because the
turning solve's answer is **not level-independent**: on the same section, hitting
10 degrees lands on 0.0001297 m at `n_side` 45 and 0.0001570 m at `n_side` 58.

### Memory, measured not forecast

Peak RSS for `oh_L3` on 6 ranks: **5,888,748 KB over 567,256 cells = 10,632
bytes/cell**, against the 10,620 the forecasts assumed. The probe stayed in ANK;
Newton–Krylov allocates a 20-vector subspace plus ILU on top, so **these are a
floor, not a ceiling**.

## 8. What is NOT established

1. **Nothing is grid-converged.** CDp moved 5.2 % and CMy 18 % from one mesh
   respacing. No drag or moment figure here may be quoted as converged — and that
   includes the neutral point, which is now a design input.
2. **The alpha 4 Newton–Krylov stall is undiagnosed.** Two hypotheses refuted.
3. **The cause of folded cells on 15 % of the design space is undiagnosed.** Two
   hypotheses refuted.
4. **Leading-edge resolution is operating-point dependent** and the level
   definitions do not express it.
5. **Which of the 2026-09-04 fixes moved cp cannot be separated** — the lift axis
   and the LE respacing land in the same solution.
6. **Tip-cap viscous quantities are not quotable.** The cap's wall normal *is*
   the spanwise direction; measured cap y+ reaches 4.2 against the OML's 0.9.
7. **No comparison against experiment exists anywhere in S8.**
8. The hold-out set `round_c_lhs10_seed42` remains locked and untouched.

## 9. Readiness verdict

**Ready for desktop: yes, for grid convergence on index 83. No, for a
design-space campaign.**

The distinction matters and is the point of this audit.

**What makes the grid-convergence job ready.** The family is uniformly refined
and verified so (1.29–1.31 in all three directions and all three spacings). All
four levels build with zero inverted cells. The coarse level is already solved,
so only two or three levels remain. The mesh on index 83 is clean by every gate.
The convergence gate reads outputs, not just a residual, and distinguishes a
converged solution from a frozen solver. The reference contract is verified
identical across runs. An independent method agrees on lift to 1.9 % at the
design point and on neutral point to 3.7 % of MAC.

**What blocks the campaign.** 15 % of the design space produces folded cells from
an undiagnosed cause. The build now refuses to ship them, which is the safety
property that mattered, but a mesher that fails on one design in seven is not an
automation path. That is a *meshing* problem and it does not block index 83.

**Recommended order:** run grid convergence on index 83 (C→M→F) while the fold
cause is chased in parallel on the laptop, since meshing costs 12–43 s and CFD
costs hours.

**Sizing caution.** `gci_FF` needs ≥ 44.6 GiB and that is a floor. Three levels
(C/M/F, topping at 22.0 GiB) is already a valid GCI; the fourth buys confirmation
that the order is asymptotic. Confirm the host's RAM before committing to four.

## 10. Questions a reviewer should press on

1. **Is holding y+ variable across the family right?** s0 scales by 1/r, so the
   whole mesh refines uniformly, but y+ falls between levels (measured p95 0.5573
   at `gci_C`). The alternative — hold s0 fixed and refine only tangentially —
   isolates everything but the wall. Which is the defensible choice for a
   wall-resolved RANS GCI?
2. **Is r = 1.3 with 4 levels the right family**, or is 3 levels enough given the
   fourth costs 44.6 GiB and roughly 4× the solve time of the third?
3. **Is `ACCEPTED_SOLVER_FROZEN` a legitimate category?** Alpha 4's forces are
   stable to 9.2e-09 but its residual is frozen at 7.242 of 8 orders and the
   solver is taking null steps. Should such a point enter a lift curve at all,
   even flagged?
4. **The trailing-edge corner takes 86–106 deg of turning in one cell.** It is a
   C0 kink so refinement cannot help, and it costs 0.01–0.2 % of CDp. Is
   accepting that correct, or does the blunt base need a small radius — which
   would change the governed geometry?
5. **Does the leading-edge cp excess at alpha 8 (36 cells, peak +0.46)
   invalidate the high-load point** for anything beyond the pressure bound, given
   it moves CDp by only 0.27 %?
6. **Is an algebraic march defensible against a hyperbolic one** for a paper, given
   it was chosen because pyHyp segmentation-faulted 25 times and produced no
   diagnosis?
7. **Folded cells on 15 % of designs**: is it acceptable to publish a
   grid-convergence result on one clean geometry while that is unresolved,
   provided it is disclosed?
