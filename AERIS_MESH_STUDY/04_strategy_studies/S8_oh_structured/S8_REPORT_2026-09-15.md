## 0. The one-page summary

**What AERIS needs.** AERIS designs a small blended-wing-body (BWB) drone: a flying wing, 1.5–2.5 m
tip to tip, cruising at 28 m/s at 1,500 m. The design space has 20 variables (chords, span,
sweeps, twist, dihedral, elevon). To explore it, the project needs aerodynamic forces for
many designs. The fast tools it already has (AVL, a vortex-lattice code, and NeuralFoil, a
learned airfoil model) are approximate. High-fidelity CFD is accurate but slow. The plan is
**multi-fidelity**: run CFD on a well-chosen set of designs, learn how the fast tools are
wrong, and correct them everywhere else.

**What S8 is.** S8 is the eighth strategy the project tried for turning a design into a
trustworthy CFD result without a human in the loop. It is a chain:

> design vector → wing surface (pyGeo loft) → **our own O-H structured mesh** → ADflow RANS
> solve (Spalart–Allmaras turbulence model) → convergence judgement → dataset row → audit

**What works, and is verified.**
- Meshing: **100 of 100 designs** mesh with zero folded cells, bit-identically on rebuild,
  with the wall exactly on the design surface (2.2e-16 m).
- The solver setup: friction drag within **0.3–0.6 %** of NASA's agreed answer (NACA 0012). On
  the one public case at our Reynolds number (NACA 4412) it matches NASA's reference code on
  friction, separation point and velocity profiles.
- The convergence rules: every equation is judged separately; iterative error sits hundreds of
  times below the effects being measured.
- The pilot: ten wings, four angles, at a coarse level (567k cells) and five wings at a
  medium level (1.1M cells) — 69 archived results.
- The multi-fidelity idea: AVL plus a learned offset predicts CFD lift on a wing it has not
  seen to rms 0.004, and the neutral point to 2.8 mm.

**What is not established — the honest part.**
- **Drag is not grid-converged.** Coarse → medium moves drag by −26 to −34 drag counts
  (13–16 %), almost all of it pressure drag. Halving or doubling the artificial dissipation
  moves coarse-mesh drag by −21 / +35 counts, and refining the trailing-edge spacing by about
  −10. The coarse mesh's drag is substantially a numerical artefact, and the fine levels that
  would show where it converges need the cloud.
- **The archive fails the rule we adopted on 13 September** (y+ ≤ 1 on every wall cell): the old
  tip cap reached y+ 4.2. All 69 rows must be re-run. The re-run is approved, but on hold until
  the settings are decided (below).
- **Model-form error is only bounded, not measured**: fully turbulent SA could overstate
  profile drag 1.4–2× if the real wing keeps laminar flow; no second turbulence model runs yet.
- **No experiment on an AERIS-like wing exists.** Validation is a chain of evidence, not a
  number.

**What happens next.** Tonight's queue tests the settings that move drag most: matrix
dissipation, whether the dissipation effect shrinks on a finer mesh, trailing-edge spacing on
the medium mesh, and a clean far-field test. Tomorrow the PI decides the settings. Then the
re-run (~17 h), then the cloud batch for the fine levels, then a real grid-convergence study
and the multi-fidelity model.

## 1. Background

### 1.1 AERIS and the operating point

| Quantity | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Speed / altitude | 28 m/s at 1,500 m, ISA atmosphere | `mission_authority_v1.yaml` |
| Temperature, density, viscosity | 278.4 K, 1.0581 kg/m³, 1.7419e-5 Pa·s | same |
| Mach number | **0.0837** — essentially incompressible | same |
| Reynolds number | **1.53 million on a 0.9 m reference chord** (1.70 million per metre) | same |
| Angles of attack in the dataset | −2°, 0°, 4°, 8° | same |
| Sideslip, rates, controls | all zero | same |

The Reynolds number, `Re = ρVL/μ`, says how much inertia dominates viscosity. It decides the
boundary-layer thickness and whether the flow is laminar or turbulent. Each design's own
Reynolds number on its mean aerodynamic chord runs **0.78–1.21 million**; since 13 September
every dataset row records it.

### 1.2 CFD in twenty minutes

**The equations.** Air obeys the Navier–Stokes equations: conservation of mass, momentum (three
components) and energy. Solving them directly for turbulent flow at Re 1.5 million is far too
expensive, so engineering CFD uses **RANS** (Reynolds-Averaged Navier–Stokes). It solves for the
time-averaged flow and represents the effect of turbulence with a **turbulence model**. S8 uses
**Spalart–Allmaras (SA)**, a one-equation model built for wings and the most-used model in
external aerodynamics. So every S8 solve has six coupled equations: continuity, three momentum,
energy, and SA's turbulence variable ν̃ ("nu-tilde").

**The mesh.** The equations are solved on a **mesh** (or grid): the space around the wing is
cut into small cells, and the solver balances the flow through each one. Two kinds matter:
- *Structured* meshes are logically rectangular blocks, indexed (i, j, k). They are efficient
  and accurate, but hard to generate automatically around a complex shape.
- *Unstructured* meshes use arbitrary cells. They are easy to generate and less efficient.

S8 is structured.

**The boundary layer and y+.** Next to the wing surface the velocity drops to zero in a thin
**boundary layer**, a few millimetres here. Friction drag is decided inside it. To resolve it
without shortcuts ("wall functions"), the first cell must sit in the *viscous sublayer*:
**y+ ≤ 1**. y+ is the wall distance scaled by the friction velocity and viscosity. Above about 5
a cell sits in the *buffer layer*, where the turbulence model's wall behaviour is not
calibrated. S8 is a **wall-resolved** mesh: y+ ≤ 1 on every wall cell is mandatory (PI
decision, 13 September).

**Drag, and its two parts.** Force coefficients are forces divided by dynamic pressure and a
reference area: CL (lift), CD (drag), CMy (pitching moment). Drag splits into
- **pressure drag CDp** — pressure pushing on the surface; includes induced drag from the tip
  vortices and form drag from the boundary layer's displacement;
- **friction drag CDv** — shear stress along the surface.

A **drag count** is 0.0001 in CD. Our wings' CD is about 180–210 counts, so one count is about
0.5 %. International drag-prediction workshops find codes scatter by 4–5 counts on the same
case.

**Convergence: are the equations solved?** The solver iterates. After each iteration the
**residual** measures how far the discrete equations are from balanced; convergence means the
residuals have fallen by many orders of magnitude *and* the forces have stopped changing. The
remaining **iterative error** must be far smaller than the effects being measured.

**Three kinds of error.** Every CFD number carries
1. **iterative error** — the equations were not fully solved (controlled by convergence rules);
2. **discretization error** — the mesh is too coarse to represent the flow (controlled by
   refining the mesh);
3. **model error** — RANS and SA are approximations of real turbulence, and transition from
   laminar to turbulent flow is not modelled at all (controlled only by comparing with
   experiment or other models).

**Grid convergence (GCI).** To measure discretization error, solve the same case on a
*family* of systematically refined meshes: same topology, every cell spacing divided by a
**refinement ratio r**, here r = 1.3. With three levels, **Richardson extrapolation** estimates
the answer on an infinitely fine mesh and the **observed order p** at which the error shrinks.
The **Grid Convergence Index** (Celik et al. 2008) turns that into an uncertainty band. With
two levels you only get a *trend*, not a band. S8 has reached two levels on the desktop; the
third and fourth need the cloud.

**Verification and validation.** *Verification*: are we solving the equations right? Compare
with known answers — other codes, analytic solutions, grid families. *Validation*: are they the
right equations? Compare with experiment. They are different questions, and a code can pass the
first and fail the second.

**Numerical (artificial) dissipation.** ADflow's flux scheme is *central* differencing, which
on its own is unstable. So it adds a small artificial smoothing term, controlled by
coefficients `vis2` and `vis4` with "scalar dissipation" by default. That smoothing acts like
extra viscosity. On a coarse mesh it can create spurious pressure drag, and it vanishes only
as the mesh is refined. This became central on 15 September (§4, G5).

### 1.3 Why automated meshing of a BWB is hard, and what came before S8

The project runs a governed "tournament" of meshing strategies (`ADR-0011`): each built
independently, judged on frozen metrics, a hold-out set of ten designs locked until the end.

| Strategy | Idea | Why it was not the answer |
|---|---|---|
| S0–S5 | tip-first marching, cross-field layouts, station sweeps, multiblock, frozen RBF | the Stage-02 versions shared one tip closure and "compared one topology wearing four labels"; rebuilt independently under ADR-0011 |
| S2 | cross-field quad layout | excellent on one geometry; 27/31/37/39 blocks across six designs — **not deterministic** |
| S6 / S7 | the "C family": six blocks per section with leading- and trailing-edge collars, pyHyp hyperbolic marching; S7 added an unstructured Gmsh/SU2 route | reached CFD, but the nose collar starved leading-edge resolution: **25.7–37.9° of surface turning per cell** against a conventional 10°; surface cp rose to **5.3** on candidate C03, physically impossible (bound ~1.002); that strip carried ~80 % of pressure drag. Found in all 65 surface meshes of the study |
| "D family" | fix the collar inside that topology | reached 10.6° per cell, then pyHyp **segmentation-faulted 25 times** — the collar size also set the trailing-edge and tip-cap point counts |

On 4 September the PI directed: *"i want to debug why the cfd doesnt converge. is it because of
the mesh or the solver? propably the mesh. so we shoudld change our meshing approach"* and
*"i want to think like an anegineer and make it work with y+<1"*. **S8 was the answer**: an
O-H structured grid after Zhang et al. (2026), with no collars, meshed by an algebraic march
that cannot crash (`ADR-0018`).

## 2. How the S8 pipeline works

### 2.1 Geometry: from 20 numbers to a wing surface

**The design vector** (`configs/geometry/bwb.yaml`). Semi-span means root to one tip.

| Group | Variable | Range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planform | `c1_m` | 0.70–1.10 m | root chord |
| | `c2_ratio`, `c3_ratio`, `c4_ratio` | 0.55–0.80, 0.30–0.55, 0.08–0.20 | chords at span breaks b1, b2 and the tip, over root chord |
| | `b_total_m` | 0.75–1.25 m | **semi-span** |
| | `b3_ratio` | 0.40–0.55 | outer-panel span fraction |
| | `split_ratio` | 0.40–0.60 | inner/outer leading-edge spline split |
| | `sw1_deg`, `sw2_deg`, `sw3_deg` | 20–40°, 15–35°, 5–25° | leading-edge sweep, inner / mid / outer |
| Twist | `twist_b0..b3_deg` | −1…1°, −3…0°, −5…−1°, −8…−2° | incidence at root and span breaks; negative outboard is **washout** |
| Dihedral | `dihedral_b1..b3_deg` | 0 (pinned), 0–6°, 0–10° | root panel held flat |
| Elevon | `elevon_start/end/hinge_frac` | 0.50–0.70, 0.85–0.98, 0.65–0.82 | control surface (undeflected in all CFD) |

Airfoils are fixed per span station: **MH91** at root and first break, **E374** at the second,
**NLF1015** at the tip, blended in between. Twist and dihedral vary piecewise-linearly between
the four stations.

**Design sets.** `lhs100_seed42`: 100 designs by Latin-hypercube sampling, for development.
`round_c_lhs10_seed42`: **the hold-out, never touched**. The **ten pilot geometries**
(12, 13, 16, 23, 29, 36, 47, 65, 81, 83) were chosen by maximin over the normalised design
variables, seeded with the reference design 83 and former folding designs. Their coverage
distance is 1.69, against a random-ten median of 1.78. Design **83** is the reference design
behind most single-geometry studies.

**The loft.** The generator builds 17 section records; pyGeo lofts a smooth B-spline surface
through them. The CFD surface opens the sharp trailing edge symmetrically to a blunt base of
0.5 % of chord (1 mm minimum), which structured meshing needs. The low-fidelity AVL model is
written from sections *extracted* from the same loft, so AVL and CFD fly the same wing. On 15
September AVL's sections were measured within 0.03–0.13 % of chord of the loft.

### 2.2 The mesh: O-H topology, explained

Picture slicing the wing at one spanwise station: you see an airfoil. Around it S8 builds an
**O-grid**: one closed ring of points hugging the airfoil (trailing edge → upper surface →
leading edge → lower surface → trailing edge), then rings marching outwards like tree-trunk
growth rings, to a circle 40 root chords away. Stack those O-slices along the span and you have
the **H** direction: root symmetry plane → tip → beyond the tip. "O-H" = O around the section,
H along the span.

**Three volume blocks** (sizes are cells at the coarse level `gci_C`):

| Block | What | Cells |
|---|---|---|
| `o_wing` | the O-slices from root to tip, 92 around × 64 outwards × 48 spanwise | 282,624 |
| `o_out` | the same O-slices continued beyond the tip to the spanwise far field | 276,736 |
| `cap_out` | a thin H-block closing the space in front of the flat tip face | 7,896 |

**Two surface blocks** make the whole wing wall: the wing skin, and the **tip cap**. The tip cap
is a single H-patch whose outline *is* the airfoil-shaped tip section, node for node. The C
family needed thirteen surface blocks. Block faces meet node-to-node (14 connections, none
open), and the O-ring's first and last points are shared, so the ring closes without a seam
gap.

**How a mesh is built** (`build_volume.py`, `march_o.py`, `strategy_s8.py`):
1. **Section ring.** Points around each section are spaced by two-sided hyperbolic (Vinokur)
   stretching and re-evaluated on the exact loft. Leading-edge spacing is *solved for*: the ring
   is refined until each cell turns the surface by at most **10°**. Trailing-edge spacing is
   `ds_te_frac` = 0.4 % of chord.
2. **Spanwise smoothing.** Leading-edge spacing may grow by at most 1.15× between neighbouring
   stations (defect 15 had a 2.6× jump).
3. **Span law.** Spanwise spacing clusters at the tip, so the tip cap's first cell is
   `tip_span_first_cell_in_s0` × s0 (2 since 13 September; it was 10).
4. **Wall-normal march.** An algebraic march in the section plane blends from the wall normal to
   a radial direction, with Laplacian smoothing that grows away from the wall. pyHyp was
   deliberately not used: it crashed 25 times on the D family. An algebraic march cannot crash,
   and every cell can be checked afterwards. First-cell height s0 is `s0_frac` 3.6e-6 of the
   bounding-box diagonal (4.73e-6 m on design 83); 65 layers; far field 40 root chords.
5. **Marching frame.** Each section marches in a plane square to the span (`span_normal`). An
   earlier best-fit (SVD) plane tilted up to 8°, and 40 m out that tilt folded cells on 15 % of
   designs (defect 20). Each node keeps its own out-of-plane offset, so the wall stays exactly
   on the loft (defect 21).
6. **Validity.** Every assembled hexahedron's volume is checked. If any is negative, the build
   climbs a smoothing ladder; if the ladder is exhausted, **the build fails** rather than writing
   a bad grid (defect 19).
7. **Guards.** The wall layer must equal the loft to 1e-9 m. The tip-cap cell must not exceed
   3× s0. The mesh is written to CGNS for ADflow.

Meshing costs about **12 seconds per design**. Solving costs hours. So mesher speed is never the
bottleneck.

**The grid family** (one refinement ratio, `refined_level`, r = 1.3 in each direction inside
`o_wing`):

|---|---|---|---|
| `gci_C` (coarse, = `oh_L3`) | 567,256 | desktop | ~6.8 GiB |

### 2.3 The solver: ADflow, and every setting in force

ADflow is the University of Michigan MDO Lab's structured, cell-centred, finite-volume RANS code
(part of the MACH-Aero framework).

| Setting | Value | Why / evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Equations, model | RANS, Spalart–Allmaras (with the ft2 term) | standard for attached wing flow |
| Lift direction | `liftIndex 3` (z up) on AERIS meshes; checked by reading the freestream direction back **before** every solve | defect 14: the default of 2 applied angle of attack as sideslip |
| Nonlinear solver | **ANK only** (approximate Newton–Krylov, `--no-nk`), `ANKSubspaceSize` 10, `ANKPCILUFill` 1 | full Newton–Krylov froze at 1.1M cells with lean Krylov settings; ANK reproduced NK results to 5 decimals |
| Multigrid | single grid (`sg`) | 2-level multigrid 1.8× slower; 3-level impossible on O-H topology (B5) |
| Stopping rule | total residual down to **1e-6** relative; cap 30,000 cycles; time limit | 1e-8 bought nothing measurable (B2) |
| Spatial scheme | central differencing with **scalar** artificial dissipation, `vis2` 0.25, `vis4` 0.0156 (ADflow defaults) | **under test**: vis4 moves coarse drag by −21/+35 counts |
| Turbulence advection | first order (ADflow default) | second order changed CD −0.25 % (B8) |
| Freestream turbulence | archive: ADflow default `eddyVisInfRatio` 0.009 (SA χ ≈ 1.3). **Adopted for the re-run: 0.21 (χ = 3)**, NASA TMR's prescription | CD +0.88 % mean (range +0.46…+1.19 %), CL unchanged |
| Low-speed preconditioner | off | tested 15 Sept: no effect on the converged answer |
| Reference area | each geometry's **own** half-area (`reference_areas.json`) | defect 23 |
| Moment reference, chord | (0.40, 0, 0) m, 0.9 m, for every design | convention; see stability caveat (§8.3) |
| Half model | symmetry plane at the root, β = 0 | root plane flat to 8e-18 m |
| Ranks | 6 MPI ranks on the desktop | time-to-answer 827 s at 6 ranks for `gci_C` α 0 |

### 2.4 Is a run acceptable? The layered judgement

A run is accepted only if all of these hold:

1. **Solver guards** (`solve_s8.py`). At least 10 iterations; every force finite and inside
   physical bounds (e.g. 0 ≤ CD ≤ 0.5); the freestream direction and lift index read back
   and correct.
2. **Convergence gate** (`convergence_gate.py`), two routes:
   - *route 1*: the residual reached the target and the run was healthy;
   - *route 2*: ≥ 5 orders of residual drop, **and** forces settled over an adaptive tail
     window (CL < 0.05 %, CD < 0.1 %, |ΔCMy| < 1e-4 absolute, because CMy crosses zero), **and**
     no divergence or growing oscillation.

   A **frozen solver** — forces flat because the solver stopped stepping — is reported separately
   (`ACCEPTED_SOLVER_FROZEN`) and excluded from grid-convergence work.
3. **Per-equation convergence, Fluent's way** (12 Sept, PI direction). Each of the six equations
   is normalised by its largest residual in the first five iterations. Continuity, momentum and
   turbulence must each fall by 3 orders, energy by 6 (Fluent's defaults).
4. **Archive audit** (`audit_runs.py`), on every dataset row:
   - gate verdict and per-equation orders;
   - residual ≤ 1e-6;
   - the geometry's own reference area;
   - moment reference and chord;
   - zero folded cells, and the grid on the design surface;
   - **worst-cell y+ ≤ 1**;
   - ≥ 10 iterations;
   - freestream direction;
   - since 15 Sept, **friction plausibility**: CDv between 0.6 and 1.6 × a turbulent flat plate
     at the design's Reynolds number.

   That last check exists because a broken turbulence-model variant passed everything else.

### 2.5 From run to dataset

`dataset_row.py` writes one row per accepted solve with 53+ fields:
- identity and hashes;
- the design vector;
- mesher, level and cell count;
- git commit and solver options *as read back from the solver*;
- operating point and each design's own Reynolds numbers;
- CL, CD, CDp, CDv, CMx/y/z;
- every convergence measure;
- mesh state, y+ percentiles, cp-bound checks;
- paths to surface and volume fields (for future AI field prediction);
- the paired AVL result.

`collect_dataset.py` builds `05_s6_cfd_qualification/dataset/` (69 rows today) and refuses
unjudged or rejected runs.

### 2.6 Orchestration: how runs are launched and protected

- `run_campaign.py` runs a pilot level over the ten geometries. It holds an exclusive lock (one
  measurement at a time), passes each geometry's own area, refuses a geometry without one, and
  runs a regression check first.
- **Queues** (`artifacts/s8_checklist/queueNN.sh`) chain long test sequences overnight:
  - they skip any run that already has a result, so a crash loses nothing;
  - they kill a run cleanly if free memory falls below ~2 GiB, or at a time limit;
  - a **decision gate** stops before expensive steps if an earlier test moved drag by ≥ 1 count.
- `cloud_preflight.py` and `cloud_run.sh` are the cloud path. Preflight checks reference areas,
  memory, environment and authorization before anything is billed. The run builds meshes in the
  cloud (12 s each) rather than uploading them, and ends with the archive audit.
- `select_levels.py` picks grid levels from *available* memory, not installed memory.

### 2.7 The multi-fidelity idea, in one paragraph

AVL, a vortex-lattice method, is inviscid and thin, but it takes seconds. CFD takes hours. On
the ten pilot wings, AVL's lift differs from CFD's by a nearly **constant offset at each angle of
attack**. So the plan: learn that offset from the CFD designs, add it to AVL everywhere. Tested
by leaving one wing out, ten times (D6):
- lift error falls from rms 0.022 to **0.004**;
- the neutral point from 20 mm to **2.8 mm**.

Drag needs its own model, since AVL gives only induced drag. The part AVL cannot supply varies
only 3.8–4.7 % between wings at each angle, which a simple model can carry.

## 3. Chronology

Dates are 2026. Commit titles are in `git log`; this is the story they tell.

### Before S8 — 30 August to 3 September: the S6 qualification

A governed qualification scaffold (M0–M2 gates, mission authority, classification thresholds)
and one measured CFD "canary" on the C-family candidate C03. It ran out of memory twice and
completed on 2 September. The postmortem found an interface defect traced to the leading-edge
collar, starved wrap resolution in all 65 meshes, and a pyHyp marching envelope that failed at
the tip cap. On 3 September the PI asked *"in the general roadmap where are we? and why we
failed?"* and set the pace: *"we must be able to produce the paper within this month."*

### Phase 1 — 4–5 September: S8 is born and survives its first defects

- **First S8 grid and first CFD point** (ADR-0018).
- **Defect 14 — the lift axis.** `liftIndex` was omitted, so ADflow applied the angle of attack
  as **sideslip**; every early result was void. Caught because α 8 reported a stagnation cp of
  0.007. Fixed, and now checked before every solve (ADR-0019), together with defect 15
  (leading-edge spacing jumping 2.6× between stations).
- **The corrected α sweep** on design 83: three angles converged, α 4 froze (Newton step 0,
  linear residual 1.000).
- **An output-based convergence gate** replaced a bare 1e-8 residual cutoff: forces settled *and*
  residual drop *and* health, with frozen solvers flagged.
- **AVL on the identical loft.** Lift agreed to 1.9 % at α 8. A "stability disagreement" was
  two sign errors of the assistant's own (defect 17), caught by the PI noticing mirror-image
  moment curves.
- **100-design robustness screen.** 15 designs folded cells. Defect 19: the fold check had been
  2D per section, and folds were *between* sections. Mechanism found: the marching plane
  tilted up to 8° (defect 20). Fix: span-normal marching. Its first version moved the wing
  surface by 1.2 mm while every quality metric stayed unchanged (defect 21), so the wall offset
  was restored and guarded. **Result: 100/100 designs clean.**
- **The grid family** is generated from one ratio (r = 1.3) instead of by hand; its first
  audit for external review corrected two overclaims.

### Phase 2 — 6 September: the desktop campaign plan and the first refinement

- `PLAN_desktop_campaign.md`, revised after an external review; authorized as ADR-0020 and policy
  `s8_campaign_v1` for **two levels only** (the host cannot hold a third — "a trend, not a GCI.
  Say so").

## 5. The rulebook

### 5.1 Scales to keep in mind

| Quantity | Size |
|---|---|
| One drag count | 1e-4 CD ≈ **0.5 %** of our CD |
| Cross-code scatter at drag workshops | 4–5 counts |
| Path noise of the 1e-6 rule | 0.32 % of CDp ≈ 0.6 counts |
| Decision threshold used by the queues | **1 count** |
| Coarse → medium drag change | 26–34 counts |
| Dissipation sensitivity (vis4 ×½ / ×2) | −21 / +35 counts |

### 5.2 Operating point and references

Given in §1.1. Areas: each geometry's own half-area from the loft, 0.298–0.516 m²; mesh-derived
areas agree within 0.21 %. Moment reference x = 0.40 m, chord 0.9 m for all designs.

## 6. Safeguards and fallbacks

### 6.1 Checks that fail closed — they stop the run rather than warn

| Guard | What it prevents | Born from |
|---|---|---|
| Freestream direction and lift index read back before solving | angle of attack silently applied as sideslip | defect 14 |
| Wall layer within 1e-9 m of the loft | a wing silently deformed by a mesh "fix" | defect 21 |
| Signed volume of every assembled hex, smoothing ladder, then raise | writing a folded mesh and exiting 0 | defect 19 |
| Tip-cap cell ≤ 3 × s0 | a wall that is not wall-resolved | tip cap, 13 Sept |
| Own reference area, refusal if missing (campaign, preflight, AVL pairing) | coefficients scaled by the wrong area | defect 23 |
| Minimum 10 iterations and physical force bounds | a crashed turbulence model reporting "converged" | SST, 12 Sept |
| Frozen-solver detection in the gate | flat forces from a stopped solver counted as converged | α 4, 5 Sept |
| Per-equation limits | one lagging equation hidden inside the total residual | 12 Sept |
| Archive audit incl. worst-cell y+ and friction plausibility | a defect surviving into training data | 13 and 15 Sept |
| Exclusive run lock | two measurements contaminating each other | PLAN §0.2 |
| Level selection from available memory | a job that cannot fit | 6 Sept |

### 6.2 Fallbacks — what happens when the preferred path fails

| If… | then… |
|---|---|
| Newton–Krylov freezes | ANK only (the campaign default); verification cases use stronger NK settings |
| ANK stalls (NACA cases, matrix dissipation) | NK with stronger preconditioning (`NKADPC`, more subspace, outer preconditioner iterations) |
| a mesh folds | the smoothing ladder; the old SVD frame stays selectable; failure raises |
| the host cannot hold a third level | `gci_MF` (a half-step) on 14–26 GiB hosts; below 14 GiB, two levels reported as a **trend**; otherwise the cloud |
| a run crashes or the machine restarts | queues are resumable; completed runs are never repeated |
| memory runs low | the queue kills the run cleanly below ~2 GiB free |
| a test moves drag by ≥ 1 count | the queue stops before the expensive step and waits for a decision |
| a validation claim needs more rigor | a strict residual mode, and NASA TMR cases with known answers |
| a second opinion on the solver is needed | SU2 (setup being fixed) |
| S8 ever fails on a design | the designated fallback mesher is **deformation of a validated S8 mesh**, not S6 (which carries the collar defect); both paths would be run on ~10 designs first to measure their offset |

### 6.3 Operational traps this project learned

- Never change code or run a second job during a measurement.
- Exit code 0 is not success.
- A quality metric that passes is not the metric that matters (position vs shape).
- Solver defaults are not neutral choices.
- `pkill -f` can kill your own shell.
- ADflow writes ADF-format CGNS, which h5py reads as nothing.
- The WSL VM can restart and take every job with it.
- Measure a mesh quantity on every span row, never one picked by index.
- Measure twist from the middle of the opened trailing edge.

Each is recorded in `STATE_2026-09-10.md` with its cost.

### 8.3 The challenge: weaknesses a reviewer should press on

1. **The dataset's drag is mostly a numerical quantity at the coarse level.** Three pieces of
   evidence:
   - coarse → medium removes 26–34 counts;
   - dissipation alone swings it by −21/+35;
   - trailing-edge spacing by −10.

   The offset is near-constant (±7 %) across wings, which is what makes multi-fidelity
   correction possible. But ±7 % of 28 counts is ±2 counts of wing-to-wing noise that the coarse
   data cannot resolve. Any drag ranking of designs at the coarse level must carry that.
2. **Grid convergence of drag is not demonstrated.**
   - Two levels give a trend.
   - The chordwise GCI's observed order (3.25) is too high to be trusted as asymptotic.
   - The wall-normal direction *diverges* at fixed chordwise resolution. That is plausible if
     scalar dissipation grows with cell aspect ratio, but that is a hypothesis tonight tests.
   - **Risk for the cloud:** a family whose directions converge in opposite senses may converge
     non-monotonically, and then no GCI band can be quoted. Rechecking this on `gci_F` is the
     cloud batch's first job.
3. **Model-form error dominates the unknowns.**
   - Fully turbulent SA at Re ~1 million per MAC could overstate profile drag by a median factor
     of 1.57.
   - No second turbulence model runs in ADflow.
   - SU2 is not yet trustworthy.
   - NACA 4412 shows ADflow reproduces CFL3D's SA solution, *including CFL3D's disagreement with
     the experiment*.
4. **Validation is regime-limited.**
   - The M6 is transonic.
   - NACA 0012 and 4412 are 2D.
   - No measurement exists for any BWB like AERIS.

   The honest claim is the chain in §5.7, never "S8 predicts AERIS drag to X %".
5. **Much rests on design 83 and two angles.** The directional study, the settings trials and the
   third wall-normal level were each done on one wing, usually at α 0 and 4. The pilot confirms
   robustness on ten wings, not every diagnostic.
6. **Stability verdicts are not available.** Every design would be unstable with its CG at 0.40 m,
   but 0.40 m is a convention, not a mass model. Neutral points (0.24–0.39 m) are trustworthy;
   verdicts need per-design CGs.
7. **Several thresholds are ours, not published.** They are reasoned and measured, but a reviewer
   may disagree:
   - the 1-count decision gate;
   - the friction band 0.6–1.6;
   - the 0.2° twist tolerance;
   - the 0.15 % leading-edge acceptance.
8. **The tools that measure have repeatedly been the source of error.** Examples:
   - defects 14–25;
   - on 15 September alone, five measurements (slicing, shock matching, index-picked row,
     twist corner, pressure-drag-only reading);
   - two confounded tests (tip cap inside the TE and far-field levels; layers inside the far-field
     level).

   All were caught by cross-checks, but the pattern says new analysis tools deserve an
   independent check before their numbers drive a decision. The rule now in the project's
   working notes: every run must differ from its baseline in exactly one setting, and must
   answer a question whose answer changes a decision.
9. **Authorizations were given in conversation** and written into policy by the assistant. This
   is recorded openly in each policy, but it is not a signature.
10. **The cloud cost estimate is unreliable** until a rented host is timed (the probe stage in
    `cloud_run.sh` exists for that).

### 8.4 What went well — and should be kept

- **Measure, don't assume.** The stopping rule, memory laws, rank scaling, directional
  attribution and the tip cap were each settled by a run designed for the question.
- **Retractions are recorded where the claim was made** (defect 23, the twist audit, the M6
  numbers), not quietly overwritten.
- **Guards were added at the moment each failure was understood**, so each class of failure can
  only happen once.
- **The run list is challenged against decisions**, which on 15 September cut about 17 hours of
  runs that could not change anything.

### Addendum — 16 September, 12:45: the Roe variant converges, and where its drag goes

`roe_wls_venk` is the **first** NACA 0012 variant to converge on its own criterion: drag Cauchy
satisfied at iteration 2,128, with CD varying 0.04 counts over the last 500 iterations. The
unlimited `roe_wls` never did — its recorded −0.52 % is one sample of a 29-count swing, and that
row of the table remains the least trustworthy thing in it despite looking the best.

| variant | CD | vs TMR | CDp | CDv |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TMR reference | 122.7 | — | 60.7 | 62.1 |
| ADflow, χ 3 | 128.9 | +5.0 % | 66.4 | 62.4 |
| SU2 JST (`wing`) | 136.8 | +11.5 % | 65.2 | 71.7 |
| SU2 `wls` | 137.3 | +11.9 % | 65.5 | 71.8 |
| SU2 `roe_wls_venk` | **150.5** | **+22.7 %** | **87.2** | **63.4** |

So the Roe flux with MUSCL and the Venkatakrishnan limiter (constant 0.05, entropy fix 0.001,
both read from the solver's own log) **fixes the friction**: CDv +2.1 % where every JST variant
sits at +15.5 %, exactly as the flat-plate result predicted. And it spends that gain twice over
on pressure drag.

**Where the pressure drag goes.** The surface cp was recovered from the conservative variables
in `surface.csv` — p = (γ−1)[E − ½(mₓ²+m_y²)/ρ], cp = (p−p∞)/q∞ with the freestream the solver
printed — and checked before use: stagnation cp +1.011 on the lower surface, suction peak −5.663
at x/c 0.0024 against CFL3D's −5.666 at 0.0027, and cp +0.183 at the trailing edge against
CFL3D's +0.188. Panel-integrating that distribution returns CL +1.1018 and CDp 87.20 counts
against SU2's own +1.1020 and 87.19 — so SU2's number is right and the distribution is right.

The same integration applied to CFL3D's and FUN3D's own published cp returns CDp 60.66 and 60.68
against the TMR table's 60.67. The method is sound to 0.01 counts.

Cumulative CDp along the chord, against `wls` on the identical mesh:

| x/c ≤ | `roe_wls_venk` | `wls` | CFL3D finest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.001 | −389.5 | −392.7 | −404.5 |
| 0.01 | −938.8 | −945.2 | −952.4 |
| 0.05 | −1138.7 | −1146.7 | −1144.7 |
| 0.3 | −586.1 | −596.7 | −589.6 |
| 0.6 | −85.6 | −100.9 | −98.2 |
| TE | **+87.2** | **+65.5** | **+60.7** |

The excess is **not** at the leading edge. It accumulates smoothly along the whole chord —
+2.8 counts by x/c 0.0005, +8.5 by 0.1, +15.3 by 0.6, +21.7 at the trailing edge. The ±20-count
wobble near the nose is quadrature noise on a steep region, and `wls` lands within 4.9 counts of
CFL3D through the same quadrature, so the mesh is not the limit. This is a small, systematic,
distributed pressure error belonging to the scheme.

**The lesson is bigger than the variant.** I had argued that a cp matching the reference to 0.01
*could not* produce a 43 % pressure-drag error, and treated the pair as a contradiction to be
resolved. It is not a contradiction. That table shows CDp is a 60-count residual of contributions
reaching −1,145 counts: better than 95 % of the pressure force cancels. A systematic cp offset of
1 % is therefore worth tens of counts of drag. **Matching cp is a weak test for pressure drag at
this condition, and our own wing CDp rests on the same cancellation** — which is worth remembering
whenever a cp plot is offered as evidence that a drag number is sound.

I had called that +9.25 % "the same JST friction signature" seen on our o512 grid, and used it to

error — it distributes that error between components misleadingly, and reading a scheme's
character off one is a mistake.

**What this does to the solver comparison.** The claim that SU2's *compressible JST* path is bad
on friction stands — `wing`, `wls` and `wls_muscl_turb` all sit at CDv +15.5 % on our o512 grid,

and was still improving, against ADflow's +5.0 %. That is not like-for-like — different grids
(TMR family 897 versus our o512), different Mach treatment, and SU2's figure is an upper bound —
so it settles nothing. It does mean the head-to-head that actually matters for a 25 kg BWB at
M 0.084 has not been run yet, and the honest state is that SU2's incompressible path looks
better than anything said about it so far.
