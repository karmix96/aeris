The last 5 % of chord gives −9.2 to −10.3 counts in every one of the six cases — three different wings, two incidences, a spread of barely one count. That single band is 40–45 % of the whole difference, from 5 % of the chord. The mid-chord, 40 % of the surface, contributes ±2 counts.

Aspect ratio is preserved — which is the point

this is a pass, not a problem

A uniform refinement family should leave cell shape alone and change only cell size. It does. That is the control which says the gci_C → gci_M difference is a resolution effect and not a shape artefact — and it is why raising the chordwise count without letting edge spacing follow degrades AR (25.1 → 32.1 at the nose, measured earlier), which is the defect family 2 fixes.

What these pictures change

  • The error is an edge problem, not a chordwise-resolution problem. That is a sharper statement than the one I have been making. Refining the whole chord works because it happens to refine the edges too — 40 % of the surface contributes ±2 counts.
  • The trailing edge is the single biggest term and the most reproducible: −9.2 to −10.3 counts across three wings and two incidences. The blunt base is 0.1 % of chord with three nodes across it; that deserves a direct test.
  • This independently validates family 2's edges=True, which scales leading- and trailing-edge spacing with the chordwise count. I added it on a 7 % argument; these plots say the edges carry closer to 80 %.
  • It is not a tip or wake effect. The spanwise distribution follows area. That is another independent line against the wake hypothesis I retracted earlier.

The cheap test this suggests: hold every count fixed and refine only ds_te_frac. If the trailing-edge band is the mechanism, that recovers most of 10 counts for almost no cells.
