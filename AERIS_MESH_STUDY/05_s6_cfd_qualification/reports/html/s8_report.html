0. The one-page summary

What AERIS needs. AERIS designs a small blended-wing-body (BWB) drone: a flying wing, 1.5–2.5 m tip to tip, cruising at 28 m/s at 1,500 m. The design space has 20 variables (chords, span, sweeps, twist, dihedral, elevon). To explore it, the project needs aerodynamic forces for many designs. The fast tools it already has (AVL, a vortex-lattice code, and NeuralFoil, a learned airfoil model) are approximate. High-fidelity CFD is accurate but slow. The plan is multi-fidelity: run CFD on a well-chosen set of designs, learn how the fast tools are wrong, and correct them everywhere else.

What S8 is. S8 is the eighth strategy the project tried for turning a design into a trustworthy CFD result without a human in the loop. It is a chain:

design vector → wing surface (pyGeo loft) → our own O-H structured mesh → ADflow RANS solve (Spalart–Allmaras turbulence model) → convergence judgement → dataset row → audit

What works, and is verified.

  • Meshing: 100 of 100 designs mesh with zero folded cells, bit-identically on rebuild, with the wall exactly on the design surface (2.2e-16 m).
  • The solver setup: friction drag within 0.3–0.6 % of NASA's agreed answer (NACA 0012). On the one public case at our Reynolds number (NACA 4412) it matches NASA's reference code on friction, separation point and velocity profiles.
  • The convergence rules: every equation is judged separately; iterative error sits hundreds of times below the effects being measured.
  • The pilot: ten wings, four angles, at a coarse level (567k cells) and five wings at a medium level (1.1M cells) — 69 archived results.
  • The multi-fidelity idea: AVL plus a learned offset predicts CFD lift on a wing it has not seen to rms 0.004, and the neutral point to 2.8 mm.

What is not established — the honest part.

  • Drag is not grid-converged. Coarse → medium moves drag by −26 to −34 drag counts (13–16 %), almost all of it pressure drag. Halving or doubling the artificial dissipation moves coarse-mesh drag by −21 / +35 counts, and refining the trailing-edge spacing by about −10. The coarse mesh's drag is substantially a numerical artefact, and the fine levels that would show where it converges need the cloud.
  • The archive fails the rule we adopted on 13 September (y+ ≤ 1 on every wall cell): the old tip cap reached y+ 4.2. All 69 rows must be re-run. The re-run is approved, but on hold until the settings are decided (below).
  • Model-form error is only bounded, not measured: fully turbulent SA could overstate profile drag 1.4–2× if the real wing keeps laminar flow; no second turbulence model runs yet.
  • No experiment on an AERIS-like wing exists. Validation is a chain of evidence, not a number.

What happens next. Tonight's queue tests the settings that move drag most: matrix dissipation, whether the dissipation effect shrinks on a finer mesh, trailing-edge spacing on the medium mesh, and a clean far-field test. Tomorrow the PI decides the settings. Then the re-run (~17 h), then the cloud batch for the fine levels, then a real grid-convergence study and the multi-fidelity model.

The Reynolds number, Re = ρVL/μ, says how much inertia dominates viscosity. It decides the boundary-layer thickness and whether the flow is laminar or turbulent. Each design's own Reynolds number on its mean aerodynamic chord runs 0.78–1.21 million; since 13 September every dataset row records it.

1.2 CFD in twenty minutes

The equations. Air obeys the Navier–Stokes equations: conservation of mass, momentum (three components) and energy. Solving them directly for turbulent flow at Re 1.5 million is far too expensive, so engineering CFD uses RANS (Reynolds-Averaged Navier–Stokes). It solves for the time-averaged flow and represents the effect of turbulence with a turbulence model. S8 uses Spalart–Allmaras (SA), a one-equation model built for wings and the most-used model in external aerodynamics. So every S8 solve has six coupled equations: continuity, three momentum, energy, and SA's turbulence variable ν̃ ("nu-tilde").

The mesh. The equations are solved on a mesh (or grid): the space around the wing is cut into small cells, and the solver balances the flow through each one. Two kinds matter:

  • Structured meshes are logically rectangular blocks, indexed (i, j, k). They are efficient and accurate, but hard to generate automatically around a complex shape.
  • Unstructured meshes use arbitrary cells. They are easy to generate and less efficient.

S8 is structured.

The boundary layer and y+. Next to the wing surface the velocity drops to zero in a thin boundary layer, a few millimetres here. Friction drag is decided inside it. To resolve it without shortcuts ("wall functions"), the first cell must sit in the viscous sublayer: y+ ≤ 1. y+ is the wall distance scaled by the friction velocity and viscosity. Above about 5 a cell sits in the buffer layer, where the turbulence model's wall behaviour is not calibrated. S8 is a wall-resolved mesh: y+ ≤ 1 on every wall cell is mandatory (PI decision, 13 September).

Drag, and its two parts. Force coefficients are forces divided by dynamic pressure and a reference area: CL (lift), CD (drag), CMy (pitching moment). Drag splits into

  • pressure drag CDp — pressure pushing on the surface; includes induced drag from the tip vortices and form drag from the boundary layer's displacement;
  • friction drag CDv — shear stress along the surface.

A drag count is 0.0001 in CD. Our wings' CD is about 180–210 counts, so one count is about 0.5 %. International drag-prediction workshops find codes scatter by 4–5 counts on the same case.

Convergence: are the equations solved? The solver iterates. After each iteration the residual measures how far the discrete equations are from balanced; convergence means the residuals have fallen by many orders of magnitude and the forces have stopped changing. The remaining iterative error must be far smaller than the effects being measured.

Three kinds of error. Every CFD number carries

  1. iterative error — the equations were not fully solved (controlled by convergence rules);
  2. discretization error — the mesh is too coarse to represent the flow (controlled by refining the mesh);
  3. model error — RANS and SA are approximations of real turbulence, and transition from laminar to turbulent flow is not modelled at all (controlled only by comparing with experiment or other models).

Grid convergence (GCI). To measure discretization error, solve the same case on a family of systematically refined meshes: same topology, every cell spacing divided by a refinement ratio r, here r = 1.3. With three levels, Richardson extrapolation estimates the answer on an infinitely fine mesh and the observed order p at which the error shrinks. The Grid Convergence Index (Celik et al. 2008) turns that into an uncertainty band. With two levels you only get a trend, not a band. S8 has reached two levels on the desktop; the third and fourth need the cloud.

Verification and validation. Verification: are we solving the equations right? Compare with known answers — other codes, analytic solutions, grid families. Validation: are they the right equations? Compare with experiment. They are different questions, and a code can pass the first and fail the second.

Numerical (artificial) dissipation. ADflow's flux scheme is central differencing, which on its own is unstable. So it adds a small artificial smoothing term, controlled by coefficients vis2 and vis4 with "scalar dissipation" by default. That smoothing acts like extra viscosity. On a coarse mesh it can create spurious pressure drag, and it vanishes only as the mesh is refined. This became central on 15 September (§4, G5).

1.3 Why automated meshing of a BWB is hard, and what came before S8

The project runs a governed "tournament" of meshing strategies (ADR-0011): each built independently, judged on frozen metrics, a hold-out set of ten designs locked until the end.

S0–S5 tip-first marching, cross-field layouts, station sweeps, multiblock, frozen RBF the Stage-02 versions shared one tip closure and "compared one topology wearing four labels"; rebuilt independently under ADR-0011
S2 cross-field quad layout excellent on one geometry; 27/31/37/39 blocks across six designs — not deterministic

S8 was the answer: an O-H structured grid after Zhang et al. (2026), with no collars, meshed by an algebraic march that cannot crash (ADR-0018).

2.2 The mesh: O-H topology, explained

Picture slicing the wing at one spanwise station: you see an airfoil. Around it S8 builds an O-grid: one closed ring of points hugging the airfoil (trailing edge → upper surface → leading edge → lower surface → trailing edge), then rings marching outwards like tree-trunk growth rings, to a circle 40 root chords away. Stack those O-slices along the span and you have the H direction: root symmetry plane → tip → beyond the tip. "O-H" = O around the section, H along the span.

Three volume blocks (sizes are cells at the coarse level gci_C):

Block What Cells
o_wing the O-slices from root to tip, 92 around × 64 outwards × 48 spanwise 282,624
o_out the same O-slices continued beyond the tip to the spanwise far field 276,736
cap_out a thin H-block closing the space in front of the flat tip face 7,896

Two surface blocks make the whole wing wall: the wing skin, and the tip cap. The tip cap is a single H-patch whose outline is the airfoil-shaped tip section, node for node. The C family needed thirteen surface blocks. Block faces meet node-to-node (14 connections, none open), and the O-ring's first and last points are shared, so the ring closes without a seam gap.

How a mesh is built (build_volume.py, march_o.py, strategy_s8.py):

  1. Section ring. Points around each section are spaced by two-sided hyperbolic (Vinokur) stretching and re-evaluated on the exact loft. Leading-edge spacing is solved for: the ring is refined until each cell turns the surface by at most 10°. Trailing-edge spacing is ds_te_frac = 0.4 % of chord.
  2. Spanwise smoothing. Leading-edge spacing may grow by at most 1.15× between neighbouring stations (defect 15 had a 2.6× jump).
  3. Span law. Spanwise spacing clusters at the tip, so the tip cap's first cell is tip_span_first_cell_in_s0 × s0 (2 since 13 September; it was 10).
  4. Wall-normal march. An algebraic march in the section plane blends from the wall normal to a radial direction, with Laplacian smoothing that grows away from the wall. pyHyp was deliberately not used: it crashed 25 times on the D family. An algebraic march cannot crash, and every cell can be checked afterwards. First-cell height s0 is s0_frac 3.6e-6 of the bounding-box diagonal (4.73e-6 m on design 83); 65 layers; far field 40 root chords.
  5. Marching frame. Each section marches in a plane square to the span (span_normal). An earlier best-fit (SVD) plane tilted up to 8°, and 40 m out that tilt folded cells on 15 % of designs (defect 20). Each node keeps its own out-of-plane offset, so the wall stays exactly on the loft (defect 21).
  6. Validity. Every assembled hexahedron's volume is checked. If any is negative, the build climbs a smoothing ladder; if the ladder is exhausted, the build fails rather than writing a bad grid (defect 19).
  7. Guards. The wall layer must equal the loft to 1e-9 m. The tip-cap cell must not exceed 3× s0. The mesh is written to CGNS for ADflow.

Meshing costs about 12 seconds per design. Solving costs hours. So mesher speed is never the bottleneck.

2.4 Is a run acceptable? The layered judgement

A run is accepted only if all of these hold:

  1. Solver guards (solve_s8.py). At least 10 iterations; every force finite and inside physical bounds (e.g. 0 ≤ CD ≤ 0.5); the freestream direction and lift index read back and correct.

  2. Convergence gate (convergence_gate.py), two routes:

    • route 1: the residual reached the target and the run was healthy;
    • route 2: ≥ 5 orders of residual drop, and forces settled over an adaptive tail window (CL < 0.05 %, CD < 0.1 %, |ΔCMy| < 1e-4 absolute, because CMy crosses zero), and no divergence or growing oscillation.

    A frozen solver — forces flat because the solver stopped stepping — is reported separately (ACCEPTED_SOLVER_FROZEN) and excluded from grid-convergence work.

  3. Per-equation convergence, Fluent's way (12 Sept, PI direction). Each of the six equations is normalised by its largest residual in the first five iterations. Continuity, momentum and turbulence must each fall by 3 orders, energy by 6 (Fluent's defaults).

  4. Archive audit (audit_runs.py), on every dataset row:

    • gate verdict and per-equation orders;
    • residual ≤ 1e-6;
    • the geometry's own reference area;
    • moment reference and chord;
    • zero folded cells, and the grid on the design surface;
    • worst-cell y+ ≤ 1;
    • ≥ 10 iterations;
    • freestream direction;
    • since 15 Sept, friction plausibility: CDv between 0.6 and 1.6 × a turbulent flat plate at the design's Reynolds number.

    That last check exists because a broken turbulence-model variant passed everything else.

2.6 Orchestration: how runs are launched and protected

  • run_campaign.py runs a pilot level over the ten geometries. It holds an exclusive lock (one measurement at a time), passes each geometry's own area, refuses a geometry without one, and runs a regression check first.
  • Queues (artifacts/s8_checklist/queueNN.sh) chain long test sequences overnight:
    • they skip any run that already has a result, so a crash loses nothing;
    • they kill a run cleanly if free memory falls below ~2 GiB, or at a time limit;
    • a decision gate stops before expensive steps if an earlier test moved drag by ≥ 1 count.
  • cloud_preflight.py and cloud_run.sh are the cloud path. Preflight checks reference areas, memory, environment and authorization before anything is billed. The run builds meshes in the cloud (12 s each) rather than uploading them, and ends with the archive audit.
  • select_levels.py picks grid levels from available memory, not installed memory.

2.7 The multi-fidelity idea, in one paragraph

AVL, a vortex-lattice method, is inviscid and thin, but it takes seconds. CFD takes hours. On the ten pilot wings, AVL's lift differs from CFD's by a nearly constant offset at each angle of attack. So the plan: learn that offset from the CFD designs, add it to AVL everywhere. Tested by leaving one wing out, ten times (D6):

  • lift error falls from rms 0.022 to 0.004;
  • the neutral point from 20 mm to 2.8 mm.

Drag needs its own model, since AVL gives only induced drag. The part AVL cannot supply varies only 3.8–4.7 % between wings at each angle, which a simple model can carry.

Before S8 — 30 August to 3 September: the S6 qualification

A governed qualification scaffold (M0–M2 gates, mission authority, classification thresholds) and one measured CFD "canary" on the C-family candidate C03. It ran out of memory twice and completed on 2 September. The postmortem found an interface defect traced to the leading-edge collar, starved wrap resolution in all 65 meshes, and a pyHyp marching envelope that failed at the tip cap. On 3 September the PI asked "in the general roadmap where are we? and why we failed?" and set the pace: "we must be able to produce the paper within this month."

Phase 1 — 4–5 September: S8 is born and survives its first defects

  • First S8 grid and first CFD point (ADR-0018).
  • Defect 14 — the lift axis. liftIndex was omitted, so ADflow applied the angle of attack as sideslip; every early result was void. Caught because α 8 reported a stagnation cp of 0.007. Fixed, and now checked before every solve (ADR-0019), together with defect 15 (leading-edge spacing jumping 2.6× between stations).
  • The corrected α sweep on design 83: three angles converged, α 4 froze (Newton step 0, linear residual 1.000).
  • An output-based convergence gate replaced a bare 1e-8 residual cutoff: forces settled and residual drop and health, with frozen solvers flagged.
  • AVL on the identical loft. Lift agreed to 1.9 % at α 8. A "stability disagreement" was two sign errors of the assistant's own (defect 17), caught by the PI noticing mirror-image moment curves.
  • 100-design robustness screen. 15 designs folded cells. Defect 19: the fold check had been 2D per section, and folds were between sections. Mechanism found: the marching plane tilted up to 8° (defect 20). Fix: span-normal marching. Its first version moved the wing surface by 1.2 mm while every quality metric stayed unchanged (defect 21), so the wall offset was restored and guarded. Result: 100/100 designs clean.

6.3 Operational traps this project learned

  • Never change code or run a second job during a measurement.
  • Exit code 0 is not success.
  • A quality metric that passes is not the metric that matters (position vs shape).
  • Solver defaults are not neutral choices.
  • pkill -f can kill your own shell.
  • ADflow writes ADF-format CGNS, which h5py reads as nothing.
  • The WSL VM can restart and take every job with it.
  • Measure a mesh quantity on every span row, never one picked by index.
  • Measure twist from the middle of the opened trailing edge.

8.3 The challenge: weaknesses a reviewer should press on

  1. The dataset's drag is mostly a numerical quantity at the coarse level. Three pieces of evidence:

    • coarse → medium removes 26–34 counts;
    • dissipation alone swings it by −21/+35;
    • trailing-edge spacing by −10.

    The offset is near-constant (±7 %) across wings, which is what makes multi-fidelity correction possible. But ±7 % of 28 counts is ±2 counts of wing-to-wing noise that the coarse data cannot resolve. Any drag ranking of designs at the coarse level must carry that.

  2. Grid convergence of drag is not demonstrated.

    • Two levels give a trend.
    • The chordwise GCI's observed order (3.25) is too high to be trusted as asymptotic.
    • The wall-normal direction diverges at fixed chordwise resolution. That is plausible if scalar dissipation grows with cell aspect ratio, but that is a hypothesis tonight tests.
    • Risk for the cloud: a family whose directions converge in opposite senses may converge non-monotonically, and then no GCI band can be quoted. Rechecking this on gci_F is the cloud batch's first job.

  3. Model-form error dominates the unknowns.

    • Fully turbulent SA at Re ~1 million per MAC could overstate profile drag by a median factor of 1.57.
    • No second turbulence model runs in ADflow.
    • SU2 is not yet trustworthy.
    • NACA 4412 shows ADflow reproduces CFL3D's SA solution, including CFL3D's disagreement with the experiment.

  4. Validation is regime-limited.

    • The M6 is transonic.
    • NACA 0012 and 4412 are 2D.
    • No measurement exists for any BWB like AERIS.

    The honest claim is the chain in §5.7, never "S8 predicts AERIS drag to X %".

  5. Much rests on design 83 and two angles. The directional study, the settings trials and the third wall-normal level were each done on one wing, usually at α 0 and 4. The pilot confirms robustness on ten wings, not every diagnostic.

  6. Stability verdicts are not available. Every design would be unstable with its CG at 0.40 m, but 0.40 m is a convention, not a mass model. Neutral points (0.24–0.39 m) are trustworthy; verdicts need per-design CGs.

  7. Several thresholds are ours, not published. They are reasoned and measured, but a reviewer may disagree:

    • the 1-count decision gate;
    • the friction band 0.6–1.6;
    • the 0.2° twist tolerance;
    • the 0.15 % leading-edge acceptance.

  8. The tools that measure have repeatedly been the source of error. Examples:

    • defects 14–25;
    • on 15 September alone, five measurements (slicing, shock matching, index-picked row, twist corner, pressure-drag-only reading);
    • two confounded tests (tip cap inside the TE and far-field levels; layers inside the far-field level).

    All were caught by cross-checks, but the pattern says new analysis tools deserve an independent check before their numbers drive a decision. The rule now in the project's working notes: every run must differ from its baseline in exactly one setting, and must answer a question whose answer changes a decision.

  9. Authorizations were given in conversation and written into policy by the assistant. This is recorded openly in each policy, but it is not a signature.

  10. The cloud cost estimate is unreliable until a rented host is timed (the probe stage in cloud_run.sh exists for that).

8.4 What went well — and should be kept

  • Measure, don't assume. The stopping rule, memory laws, rank scaling, directional attribution and the tip cap were each settled by a run designed for the question.
  • Retractions are recorded where the claim was made (defect 23, the twist audit, the M6 numbers), not quietly overwritten.
  • Guards were added at the moment each failure was understood, so each class of failure can only happen once.
  • The run list is challenged against decisions, which on 15 September cut about 17 hours of runs that could not change anything.
