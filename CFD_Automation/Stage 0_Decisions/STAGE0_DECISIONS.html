The starting numbers, and why we chose them

Every later study in the project is judged against these values: the meshes, the solver runs, the wing sets. Each row says what the value means in practice, why it was chosen, and where it comes from.

  • CD, CL, CM drag, lift and pitching-moment coefficients
  • 1 drag count = 0.0001 of CD. A wing is typically 100–300 counts
  • Mesh the grid of cells around the wing; finer = more accurate, slower
  • Residual how far the solver still is from a finished answer on one mesh
Source of each value: published from a paper or standard our choice no outside source; a starting value we may revise
D1

How accurate the answers must be

WhatValueWhat it means in practiceWhySource
Comparing two wingsΔCD ≤ 2 counts
ΔCL ≤ 0.005

The error on the difference between two wings is at most 2 counts. Wing A 0.0210, wing B 0.0205: 5 counts apart, so B is really better. Less than 2 counts apart is a tie.

We mostly pick the better wing. Two wings meshed the same way share much of their error, and it cancels in the difference.

 publishedIndustry goal ±1 count (Morrison et al.). We start at 2.
Drag of one wingmax(3 % of CD, 3 counts)

CD 0.0200 → may be off by 6 counts. CD 0.0080 → 3 % is 2.4, so the 3-count floor applies. Used against wind-tunnel data.

Expert teams with different codes disagree by about 5 counts on the same wing. One checked workflow must do better.

 publishedDPW spread σ = 0.00053 (Levy et al. 2013)
Lift of one wingmax(1 % of CL, 0.005)

CL 0.8 → up to 0.008. CL 0.3 → the floor 0.005 applies.

About the same relative size as the drag budget.

 our choice
Pitching moment0.002

CM may be off by up to 0.002. It decides pitch stability.

A starting value; checked when stability is studied.

 our choice
Turbulence-model errorown band

Every drag number is reported as "± mesh error" and, separately, "± model error". A finer mesh cannot remove model error.

Mesh error is how well we solve the equations; model error is whether the equations describe real air.

 publishedASME V&V 20-2009

D2

Which wings we test

The wing generator is new, so wings are defined by rules, not by name. Counts are per wing type (BWB, conventional).

WhatValueWhat it means in practiceWhySource
Reference wings5

Studied in depth: all mid-range; smallest area; largest area; most swept with smallest tip; most twisted. Fixed before any mesh, never swapped after a result.

A rule that works on one wing is true for one wing.

 our choice
Coverage check≤ median of 1,000 random fives

The five must cover the training set at least as well as five picked at random: no training wing far from all five.

Proves the five are spread out, not bunched.

 publishedJohnson, Moore & Ylvisaker 1990
Training set100

Spread evenly over all variables. The mesher must build all of them.

Latin hypercube: each range cut into 100 slices, each used once.

 publishedMcKay et al. 1979
Corner set42
= 34 + 8

34: each of 17 variables at its low and high end, the rest mid-range. 8: every pair of variables at all four low/high combinations. Failures stay in the list.

Shows where the rules break. 8 is the proven minimum: C(7,4) = 35 ≥ 17, while C(6,4) = 15 < 17.

 publishedKatona 1973; Kleitman & Spencer 1973
Hold-out set10, opened once

Drawn now, locked away, run once at the very end.

Looking at test wings while tuning turns them into training wings.

 publishedDwork et al. 2015, 2017
Robustness claim0 failures in ≥ 150 → ≥ 98 %

If all 150 wings work, at least ~98 % of all wings in the design space will, with 95 % confidence.

1 − 0.051/150 = 1.98 % worst failure rate. Quick rule: 3 ÷ 150.

 publishedHanley & Lippman-Hand 1983

Flow conditions and how we measure

WhatValueWhat it means in practiceWhySource
SpeedMach ≤ 0.3

Up to ~100 m/s at sea level. Air nearly incompressible. Transonic is a later stage.

Class I and II UAVs fly well below this.

 our choice
Reynolds number (mean chord)3×10⁵
1.5×10⁶
5×10⁶

How "big and fast" the wing is to the air: Re = ρ·V·c ÷ μ. A 0.2 m chord at 25 m/s, 1,500 m ≈ 3×10⁵. Wall cells must shrink as Re grows, so every mesh study runs at all three.

Low, middle, high end of small Class I and Class II. Mini/micro Class I (e.g. Raven) sit below 3×10⁵ and are left out.

 publishedISA atmosphere; Sutherland's law; aircraft data
Angles of attack0°, 4°, 8°
+12° nose & wake

Every study at 0, 4, 8. Nose and wake studies also at 12.

Cruise, climb, high lift. The nose needs finer cells as the angle grows.

 our choice
What we watchCL, CD (pressure + friction), CM, neutral point, cp, y+

A study passes only if every one passes at every angle. Good lift with bad drag is a fail.

Forces can look right while the pressure is wrong; y+ checks the first wall cell.

 our choice
Mesh family≥ 3 meshes
r ≥ 1.3

The same mesh at three sizes, each 1.3× finer in every direction: about 2.2× more cells per step.

Two meshes only show the answer moved; three show where it is heading.

 publishedCelik et al. 2008
Error barGCI × 1.25

Grid Convergence Index from the three meshes, times 1.25 to stay safe. Least-squares version if results scatter.

Standard published procedure.

 publishedCelik 2008; Roache 1994; Eça & Hoekstra 2014
Up-and-down resultsno error bar

If the answer goes up then down as the mesh gets finer, we do not quote an error bar; we find the cause.

The meshes are not yet fine enough for the method to hold.

 publishedCelik et al. 2008
Stopping ruleresidual 10⁻⁶
+ forces still
+ every equation ↓ 3 orders

A run is finished only when the residual has fallen to 10⁻⁶ of the free-stream value (ADflow L2Convergence), CL moves < 0.05 %, CD < 0.1 %, CM < 0.0001, and every equation, turbulence included, has fallen 1,000×.

Stalls before 10⁻⁶ but fell 5 orders with forces still: accepted. Otherwise: needs review. Cap 30,000 iterations or 6 h.

A low residual alone does not prove the forces stopped. Measured past 10⁻⁶: 0.12 counts, inside the 0.30 share.

Fluent's 10⁻⁶ is a scaled residual (default 10⁻³); ADflow's is a fall from free stream, stricter.

 publishedCelik 2008 App. A; ADflow docs
One change per run1

Between two compared runs only one setting changes. In a mesh study the wing never changes.

With two changes, nobody can say which moved the result.

 our choice
Turbulence modelSpalart–Allmaras, no transition

Boundary layer treated as turbulent from the nose. At low Re, drag carries a model band.

At low Re, where the flow turns turbulent sets much of the drag, and this model does not predict it.

 our choicecurrent source (Crivellini 2014) does not support it

"A study passes if …" is written before the study runs.

Weak points still open

  • Reynolds levels. The check gives Re per metre of chord, not each aircraft's real Re from its own mean chord, and mixes cruise with maximum speed. No example aircraft reaches 5×10⁶; for now it is a safety margin.
  • Numerical settings. Earlier tests moved drag 12–21 counts against a 0.45-count share. Expect this to drive the solver settings.
  • Iteration share. 0.30 counts is looser than Celik's 1/10-of-mesh-error rule (0.195). The measured 0.12 meets both; re-check once the mesh family is frozen.
  • Turbulence-model source. Crivellini 2014 found Spalart–Allmaras did fairly well at low Re, so it does not back the rule. Treat the rule as our choice until a better source is found.
  • Split of the mesh share. A plan, not a measurement. Change it only by a written decision, before new results.
