The starting numbers, and why we chose them

Every later study in the project is judged against these values: the meshes, the solver runs, the wing sets. Each row says what the value means in practice, why it was chosen, and where it comes from.

  • CD, CL, CM drag, lift and pitching-moment coefficients
  • 1 drag count = 0.0001 of CD. A wing is typically 100–300 counts
  • Mesh the grid of cells around the wing; finer = more accurate, slower
  • Residual how far the solver still is from a finished answer on one mesh
Source of each value: published from a paper or standard our choice no outside source; a starting value we may revise pending a proposal, not decided yet

How accurate the answers must be

WhatValueWhat it means in practiceWhySource
Comparing two wingsΔCD ≤ 2 counts
ΔCL ≤ 0.005

The error on the difference between two wings is at most 2 counts. Wing A 0.0210, wing B 0.0205: 5 counts apart, so B is really better. Less than 2 counts apart is a tie.

We mostly pick the better wing. Two wings meshed the same way share much of their error, and it cancels in the difference.

Since Stage 1 (2 Nov): a numerical target only. Fully turbulent SA's own error on the difference between similar wings is 4 counts median (above 2 in 71 % of pairs; 2-D strips).

 publishedIndustry goal ±1 count (Morrison et al.). We start at 2.
Drag of one wingmax(3 % of CD, 3 counts)

CD 0.0200 → may be off by 6 counts. CD 0.0080 → 3 % is 2.4, so the 3-count floor applies. Used against wind-tunnel data.

Expert teams with different codes disagree by about 5 counts on the same wing. One checked workflow must do better.

 publishedDPW spread σ = 0.00053 (Levy et al. 2013)
Lift of one wingmax(1 % of CL, 0.005)

CL 0.8 → up to 0.008. CL 0.3 → the floor 0.005 applies.

About the same relative size as the drag budget.

 our choice
Pitching moment0.002

CM may be off by up to 0.002. It decides pitch stability.

A starting value; checked when stability is studied.

 our choice
Turbulence-model errorown band

Every drag number is reported as "± mesh error" and, separately, "± model error". A finer mesh cannot remove model error.

Mesh error is how well we solve the equations; model error is whether the equations describe real air.

Stopping ruleresidual 10⁻⁶
+ forces still

A run is finished only when the residual has fallen to 10⁻⁶ of the free-stream value (ADflow L2Convergence), CL moves < 0.05 %, CD < 0.1 %, CM < 0.0001, and every equation, turbulence included, has fallen 1,000×.

Stalls before 10⁻⁶ but fell 5 orders with forces still: accepted. Otherwise: needs review. Cap 30,000 iterations or 6 h.

Weak points still open

  • Drag ranking with SA. The 2-count increment target holds for the mesh, not for the model: SA's own error on differences is about 4 counts. Ranking wings or airfoils by drag needs transition-model results (SU2 SST + LM), at least on a calibration subset.
  • Transition rests on SU2 alone. Decided 4 Nov: transition must be captured. Our ADflow build has SA only (SST aborted), so only SU2 (SST + LM) can do it. So far SU2 has run one smoke test on one ring mesh, not converged. Its cost on a full wing is unmeasured, and ADflow and SU2 SA have not yet been compared on the same mesh.
  • Mesh size. Estimated in Stage 2, the fine level has 15.2 M cells (median) with the planned rules, against the proposed 9–11 M. The band change (decided 6 Nov) gives 10.9 M, but then the short high-Re root bubbles (about 1.5 % c) get only about 3 nodes: fine for SA, not for LM. The C-H wake lines may add about +45 % on the wing blocks, so F may land nearer 15 M than 11 M. The desktop takes about 1.2 M cells: with F at 10.9 M, VC is about 1.0 M (median) and C about 2.3 M, so C does not fit on the desktop.
  • SU2 has no overset. It also refuses structured CGNS (structured meshes converted to unstructured hexes do run). So the overset wake box and vortex tube cannot serve both solvers. The C-H mesh (decided 6 Nov) has its own costs: the tip vortex must be held by the outboard blocks without a tube, the open-line march is new work, and the wake lines add cells.
  • Nose shape (decision 64): fixed on 7 Nov. A first check (Stage 2) found the upper and lower nose radius of cambered sections 3–13× apart. On 6 Nov a second check called this mostly the measuring method; that was wrong, because it compared built and raw only at the raw points, where they agree by construction. A third check with one curvature method along the surface found the real defect between the points (NACA 4415: a 0.18 % c nose cell turned 9.47° built against 4.31° true). Fixed in Stage 1; every geometry hash changed and the sets were rebuilt. The pyGeo build --all is still owed.
  • The new scope needs data. −5° to post-stall and Mach toward 0.5 lie outside the Stage 1 data (root angles 0–10°, Mach ≤ 0.2). The mesh rules were set from those ranges, so they must be checked again for the new ones.
  • Numerical settings. Earlier tests moved drag 12–21 counts against a 0.45-count share. Expect this to drive the solver settings.
  • Iteration share. 0.30 counts is looser than Celik's 1/10-of-mesh-error rule (0.195). The measured 0.12 meets both; re-check once the mesh family is frozen.
  • Still open from Stage 0: grid share 65 %, pitching moment 0.002, the iteration share, a D5 (open decisions 1, 3, 4, 6).
  • Split of the mesh share. A plan, not a measurement. Change it only by a written decision, before new results.
