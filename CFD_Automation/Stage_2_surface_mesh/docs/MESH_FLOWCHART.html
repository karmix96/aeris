What the mesher does with one wing, step by step: the rule behind each step (law), the decision that set it (D = owner decision, OPEN_DECISIONS.md), and the value at the level you pick. The rules are frozen (D82): they change only when a check fails or a solver result shows a problem.

One wing

A parameter vector (BWB, flying wing, cranked or tapered) becomes the loft: a smooth surface that gives any point from its chord fraction x/c and its station. Built at unit mean chord (similarity), with the true nose shape.

Place the stations

The first wall cell at the tip comes from y⁺ (turbulent flat plate, Cf = 0.026 Re−1/7). The last gap at the tip is 2 × that cell. Elsewhere the gap follows the local chord, with a minimum count per panel and a growth limit. A station sits exactly on every loft station, so no kink is cut.

Take the section at every station

5

Solve the nose cell

Bisection on the nose spacing until no ring cell turns the surface by more than the level's angle. It is solved at every level for its own angle; the strictest answer wins. Never coarser than the band cell. Between stations the nose cell may change by at most 1.15 ×.

Angles: VC 16.9° · C 13° · M 10° · F 7.69° · this level 10°

6

Set the cell size along the chord

One target size from the nose to the trailing edge: the nose cell (step 5); the trailing-edge cell; over the band, 0–100 % c on both sides, the band cell = bubble length ÷ cells per bubble (bubble 0.40 c at local Re 6 × 10⁴, down to 0.10 c from 4 × 10⁵); never above the largest cell; away from the nose no cell turns the surface more than the angle; cells grow by at most the growth. Upper and lower share one size field: the stricter of the two at the same arc fraction.
