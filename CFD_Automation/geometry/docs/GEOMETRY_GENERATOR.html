How the wing generator works

Eighteen numbers go in; one exact wing surface comes out, measured, labelled and checked. The same generator makes BWB and conventional wings, uses any airfoil from the AERIS database, and rebuilds published wings so the CFD can be validated against them later.

One wing, step by step

Seven steps, about 2.5 s each: design vector, planform, sections, twist and dihedral, trailing edge, loft, measure-label-check. Example: a BWB with every variable at mid-range.

Where the airfoils come from

1 · Project files

geometry/airfoils/: sections made for this project, e.g. the ONERA M6 and the RIBES scan.

2 · AERIS database

data/airfoil_database/: 2,183 sections, read by name (Selig and Lednicer files).

3 · NACA formula

Any 4- or 5-digit NACA name not found as a file is made from the formula, e.g. naca25118 for BlendedNet.

In configs/wing.yaml each station takes one name, or a list of candidates that the sampler draws evenly. A single wing can also carry its own four names.

types:
  conventional:
    airfoils: [naca4415, naca4415, [naca4412, e387], [naca0012, sc20410]]   # b0, b1, b2, b3

# one wing, its own sections
{"root_chord_m": 0.6, ..., "airfoils": ["naca2415", "naca2412", "sc20412", "naca0012"]}

Limits to know before validating

  • ONERA M6 and NASA CRM are transonic cases (Mach 0.84–0.85). Phase 1 stops at Mach 0.3, so for now they test the shape and the mesher, not the flow. RIBES is the one in-regime case (40 m/s, Re 1.43 × 10⁶).
  • CRM is approximate. Its real twist and sections vary along 21 stations; four stations and SC(2) stand-in sections are not enough to validate drag. That needs the real CRM sections and more stations.
  • BlendedNet: sections as in BlendedNet++ (the first BlendedNet used fixed CST sections whose values are not published). Twist and dihedral are not stated in the papers and are set to zero. The trailing edge of NACA 25118 follows the classic open-edge formula; OpenVSP may close it differently.
  • RIBES: the fitted section's trailing edge (0.26 % chord) is thinner than the 0.38–0.66 % measured on the model.
  • Tips are flat cuts on every wing, including the M6, whose model tip is rounded.
  • The final loft and its checks run where pyGeo is installed: python -m cfdauto.geometry.published_wings --build all.
