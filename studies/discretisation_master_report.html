Discretising AVL for a blended-wing-body design sweep

How fine must the vortex-lattice panelling and the spanwise section layout be so that a thousand-call optimiser sees the right aerodynamics — not mesh artefacts — at an affordable cost? Two pre-registered, gated studies with a reference whose legitimacy is proven, not assumed.

Blended-wing-body ISR UAV · MACH-Aero / pyGeo / AVL · 2,083 AVL solves across two studies · reference = converged AVL, not CFD · 2026-07-30

Abstract

A gradient-free optimiser will evaluate a blended-wing-body (BWB) planform thousands of times with the Athena Vortex Lattice (AVL) method. Two discretisation choices set both the cost per call and the trustworthiness of every coefficient it returns: the panel density (chordwise × spanwise vortices, and their spacing) and the section layout (where the defining airfoil stations sit along the span, and how many). We study each in isolation, with pre-registered acceptance gates that test the preservation of decisions — design ranking, trim, stability classification — not merely coefficients.

Absent CFD, the reference is a converged AVL solution whose legitimacy is demonstrated per design (grid-convergence for panels; a Richardson-converged dense-uniform reference for sections), so error is bounded as self-consistency to converged AVL — never claimed as absolute truth. Aggregation is worst-case over geometry × angle × control state; a print-noise floor guards near-zero quantities.

Findings. (i) Design ranking is robust to coarse panelling (Spearman ≥ 0.99, zero regret, zero trim/stability flips), but absolute control-surface effectiveness and induced drag are under-resolved at every affordable mesh — no panelling clears a 1% numerical-uncertainty gate. The pragmatic screening mesh is chord 16 / span-2 / uniform (c16s2u), 7.3× cheaper than production. (ii) For section layout, the entire benefit comes from pinning the hard points — control-surface edges, planform breaks, fixed-airfoil stations — which cuts median section error from 12.7% to 1.0%; information-weighted adaptive density placement never helps (0/36 designs) and is discarded. The accuracy lever is the section count, not its distribution. Both results revise earlier internal decisions on a legitimate reference.

Nomenclature & definitions

AVL
Athena Vortex Lattice — a linear vortex-lattice method (VLM); the wing is a lattice of horseshoe vortices, with a per-strip viscous profile-drag correction (NeuralFoil) here.
chordwise (Nc)
number of panels along the flow direction; resolves pressure recovery and control-surface loading.
spanwise (m)
panel multiplier across the span; strips = 2·(Nsec−1)·m. Resolves the loading distribution and, via the viscous term, profile drag.
cspace
chordwise panel spacing: 0.0 = uniform, 1.0 = full cosine (edge-clustered).
section (Nsec)
a spanwise station where the airfoil/planform is defined; AVL interpolates piecewise-linearly between sections.
c16s2u
shorthand for a mesh: chord 16, spanwise-multiplier 2, uniform spacing.
hard points
mandatory section stations: control-surface band edges, planform (sweep/taper) breaks, fixed-airfoil stations. Interpolating across them is a modelling error, not discretisation error.
GCI
Grid Convergence Index — a Richardson-extrapolation error band with safety factor Fs=1.25, refinement ratio r=2. Reported only where a quantity is monotone and asymptotic.
Richardson p
observed order of convergence fitted from three refinement levels; p≈1 ⇒ first order.
finest-grid discrepancy
honest fallback when Richardson is not licensed: the plain difference from the finest mesh actually computed.
ε
worst-case relative error on the gate quantities vs the reference — the headline accuracy number.
Spearman ρ
rank correlation of designs by L/D against the reference; ≥0.98 required, with a max-rank-displacement clause.
objective regret
how much worse the design a cheap mesh would pick performs, judged by the reference; 0% ⇒ same winner.
δtrim
−Cm/Cm,δe — elevon deflection to trim, per design per angle.
ramp fraction
control-gain ramp width ÷ elevon band width; a geometric difficulty proxy for control resolution.
equidistribution
de Boor's rule: place nodes at equal increments of a monitor ∫ρ dy, ρ ∝ |g″|1/2 for piecewise-linear interpolation error.
DoE
design of experiments — the Latin-hypercube design sweep the optimiser runs over.

1. Introduction & motivation

Conceptual design of a small BWB ISR UAV proceeds by sweeping a 19-variable planform space and ranking candidates on lift-to-drag, static margin, and control authority. The evaluation engine is AVL: cheap enough to call thousands of times, linear enough to be predictable. But "cheap" and "trustworthy" pull against each other through the discretisation. Too fine and the sweep is unaffordable; too coarse and the optimiser optimises mesh artefacts — a uniform bias in elevon power, for instance, preserves every ranking yet shifts every trim deflection, quietly moving designs across the feasibility boundary.

The design surface is discretised along two nearly independent axes, and this report treats them as two studies:

  • Panel density — the vortex-lattice resolution (chordwise, spanwise, spacing) on a fixed section layout.
  • Section positioning — where the defining sections sit and how many, on a fixed panel mesh.

Both were previously settled by an earlier internal study (2026-07-25) that recommended chord 24 / span 4 / cosine with 25 uniform sections and conditional adaptive placement. This report re-examines both on a legitimate, converged reference with decision-preserving gates, and revises both recommendations. The two studies share one methodology, described next.

2. Common methodology

2.1 Solver, geometry, and the design space

AVL is driven subprocess-wise through the MACH-Aero / pyGeo stack. The BWB is a three-segment planform (leading-edge sweeps sw1/sw2/sw3, chords c1..c4 at breaks b0..b3), four fixed-station airfoils (mh91/mh91/e374/nlf1015), one trailing-edge elevon pair whose band position and width are DoE variables, at 28 m/s, sea level, β=0. The design set is 30 Latin-hypercube "normal" designs (seed 2000) plus 6 hand-built extremes (narrow/wide elevon, high aspect ratio, strong taper, minimum tip chord, maximum sweep) that stress the corners the random sample never reaches. AVL's influence matrix is independent of angle of attack, so mesh sensitivity is weakly angle-dependent — verified, not assumed.

2.2 Ground truth without CFD — the central methodological choice

There is no CFD or wind-tunnel datum. But the geometry is analytic: pyGeo lofts an exact surface, so AVL's only discretisation error is the interpolation of a known shape. A legitimate reference is therefore a converged AVL solution — and convergence must be demonstrated, not assumed. Two constructions, one per study:

  • Panels: the reference is the finest affordable mesh (chord 48 / span 2), with a grid-convergence chain behind each quantity; where the chain is asymptotic a GCI is reported, otherwise a finest-grid discrepancy.
  • Sections: the reference is dense uniform at high count, accepted only once it is Richardson-converged in 1/N (verified <0.6% between N=151 and 201). A cross-placement "everything agrees" test was tried first and rejected as invalid — non-uniform candidates need not match a dense uniform mesh even when it is converged.
The honesty this buys, and its limit

Every error here is bounded against converged AVL, i.e. self-consistency of the discretisation. It is not a claim that AVL is reality — the VLM + strip-viscous model carries its own error (independently, elevon power runs ~1.5% short of the truth). This distinction is stated wherever an absolute number is quoted.

2.3 Quantities, angles, and the conservative control state

Nineteen quantities are frozen (drag, forces, longitudinal/lateral stability derivatives, five control derivatives, two hinge moments) plus three derived (δtrim, static margin, roll power). Angles are −2/0/+4° (the production sweep), with +2° added only where a ratio-2 convergence chain is needed. The mesh is validated at a differentially deflected control state (sym +4°, diff +4°): antisymmetric loading carries the highest spanwise wavenumber, so adequacy there is conservative for the symmetric production sweep. A dedicated transfer test (panel study Stage 6b) confirms the choice does not depend on the control state.

2.4 Aggregation, noise floor, and the gate philosophy

Whenever errors reduce to one number it is the worst case over (geometry × angle × control state) — never a mean that hides a bad corner. A print-noise floor (10−4, AVL's 6-digit output) protects near-zero quantities: below it, error is assessed as a normalised discrepancy against a frozen scale, not a runaway ratio. The gates are pre-registered and decision-oriented — they test whether a cheap discretisation keeps the design ranking, the top-k set, the trim and stability classifications, and the control-derivative signs, not merely the coefficients. A uniform 30% bias in elevon power would pass a naive coefficient test yet move designs across the trim boundary; the gates catch exactly that.

3. Study I — Panel density

1,165 AVL solves across eight gated stages establish the chordwise/spanwise/spacing budget on a fixed 25-section layout. The strip count is 2·(Nsec−1)·m with Nsec≈28–29 after control-edge snapping (a correction to the runbook's assumed 25); the hard cap is 500 strips / 6000 vortices.

3.1 Convergence and the knee

Chordwise error dominates the budget and falls steeply from chord 6 to 24, then flattens; convergence is roughly first order and the two Richardson chains disagree on most cells, so most quantities are reported by finest-grid discrepancy rather than an earned GCI. Spanwise error is ~10× smaller — span 2 is near-optimal. Uniform chordwise spacing beats cosine at every chord (the production default's cosine is the worst performer).

3.2 The seven gates, and a corrected gate 4

Five candidate meshes were carried to a 540-run ranking-and-decision test against seven pre-registered gates. Design ranking is emphatically robust — Spearman ≥ 0.99, 0% objective regret, zero trim/stability label flips, no design displaced > 4 ranks — so if ranking were all that mattered, the cheapest mesh would win. Two gates break that: numerical uncertainty < 1% on the force/moment set (gate 4) and trim-deflection preservation ≤ 0.5° (gate 6).

Audit correction (documented)

Gate 4 was originally scored from an interpolated cost/accuracy model built on four geometries; re-derived directly from the 540-run data (worst-case over 30 designs × angles), no mesh clears gate 4 — the production mesh reads ~3% worst-case, not the modelled 0.8%. The correction removes the "production is an adequate absolute-fidelity floor" reading and reinforces the escalate verdict below.

3.3 Study I verdict

Panel density — decision

No affordable mesh clears all seven gates. Ranking is safe cheaply; absolute control/drag effectiveness is under-resolved at every affordable mesh. The rule is not to lower a gate but to report the tier needs escalation. Operationally: c16s2u for ranking-driven screening (19 h, 7.3× cheaper, ranking provably intact and control-state-independent); for absolute trim/authority numbers no clean mesh exists — treat even production as ~3% optimistic and escalate the model if <1% is required.

4. Study II — Section positioning

918 AVL solves on the frozen c16s2u mesh settle where the sections go and how many. The question has real theory behind it: AVL interpolates the wing piecewise-linearly, so placing more sections where the geometry curves — de Boor equidistribution, ρ ∝ |g″|1/2, with the control-gain boxcar as a dominant channel — should reduce interpolation error. An earlier decision adopted exactly this adaptive rule. We test it against a legitimate reference.

4.1 A legitimate reference, and separability

Dense uniform is Richardson-converged (<0.6% between N=151 and 201), so uniform-201 is legitimate ground truth. Because the c16s2u vortex cap forbids N>~92, and section-placement error is shown separable from the panel mesh (the uniform→adaptive effect is 2.74% at c16s2u vs 3.04% at the cheap c8s1u), the converged reference is built where high N is affordable and the policy transfers — while the placement decision itself is re-run directly at c16s2u across all 36 designs.

4.2 The hard points do the work; adaptive density does not

An ablation isolates the two ingredients — pinning the mandatory nodes, and redistributing the free budget by curvature. At the production mesh, across all 36 designs:

Adaptive does lower the control-gain ramp fraction — it does what it is told — but equidistributing the free budget starves smooth-but-loaded regions, and the error rises. This explains the earlier "adaptive helps" result as the snapping/pinning effect misattributed to the density rule, measured then against a non-converged reference.

4.3 How many sections

With pinned uniform, typical accuracy is excellent by N=25 (median 0.64%) and N≈49 gets 90% of cells under 1%. A few control-derivative cells plateau at ~1.8–2.9% and do not improve with count — section-limited. Two quantities are irreducibly placement/print-limited: induced drag (AVL's Trefftz integral depends on the strip distribution, ~2–3%) and the symmetric hinge moment (near the print floor).

4.4 Study II verdict

Section positioning — decision

Uniform placement with pinned hard points; adaptive density off. The value is entirely in the hard points (control edges, planform breaks, fixed airfoils), which both policies now pin; curvature-based redistribution of the remainder is counterproductive. Count: N=25 for screening, ~49 when control-derivative accuracy must be tight; do not expect <1% on induced drag or hinge from any setting. This revises the earlier adaptive-placement decision on a legitimate reference.

One sentence

Run the DoE at chord 16 / span-2 / uniform panelling with 25 uniformly-spaced sections pinned to the control edges, planform breaks and fixed airfoils — ranking-accurate at 7.3× the throughput of the old production mesh — and treat absolute control-authority and induced-drag numbers as ~2–3% optimistic, escalating to higher fidelity only where a downstream decision needs them tighter.

6. Limitations & scope

  • Reference is converged AVL, not reality. Both studies bound self-consistency of the discretisation; the VLM + strip-viscous model's own error (~1.5% short on elevon power) sits underneath every absolute number.
  • Panel study: the comparator (chord 48) is itself ~1.5% short on elevon power, so gate-4 errors are optimistic; the trim gate uses a documented ±25° elevon envelope; extremes are reported separately, not pooled.
  • Section study: the "adaptive is worse" claim is scoped to the implemented monitor (magnitude-preserving curvature, floor 0.5) — the full metric horse-race and an isolation of the earlier decision's exact test were not run. The count recommendation is validated on the accuracy axis only; it should be confirmed against the trim gate. The production mesh cannot host a converged section reference (vortex cap), so the count relies on the separability-justified reduced-panel reference.
  • Both: the DoE-ranking, deflection-transfer (section), and end-to-end timing (section) gates remain future confirmatory work; the placement and panel verdicts are monotone and unlikely to move.

7. Conclusions

  1. Ranking is cheap; absolute effectiveness is not. Coarse AVL panelling preserves the design ranking and the trim/stability classification almost perfectly, but no affordable mesh resolves absolute control-surface power or induced drag to 1%. Screening and optimisation should run coarse (c16s2u); any absolute authority number needs a fidelity escalation.
  2. For section layout, geometry-complexity-driven adaptation is a red herring. The decisive act is pinning the hard points; redistributing the rest by curvature starves loaded regions and loses on every design. Uniform + pins is the policy.
  3. A legitimate reference changes the answer. Both studies revised an earlier decision, and in both cases the revision traced to a more honest reference and worst-case, decision-preserving gates — the methodological contribution as much as the numbers.
