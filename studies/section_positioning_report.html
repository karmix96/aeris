Where the wing sections go

Do we gain accuracy by clustering the defining sections where the geometry is complex — or is the answer simpler than that? A pre-registered study with a reference whose legitimacy is proven, not assumed.

Result

Place sections uniformly and pin the hard points. Do not adapt the density.

Against a legitimate converged reference, the entire benefit comes from pinning the mandatory nodes — control-surface band edges, planform breaks, and fixed-airfoil stations — which cuts worst-case section error from 21% to 5%. Information-weighted adaptive density placement makes things ~2× worse (10.8%) by starving smooth-but-loaded regions, on every one of the 14 designs. The accuracy lever is the section count, not the distribution.

This revises DECISION-0011: its "adaptive helps" result was the snapping (pinning) effect misattributed to the density rule, and was measured against a non-converged reference.

The problemSections, and the ground-truth trap

AVL interpolates the wing piecewise-linearly between defining spanwise sections. Placing them well should matter most where the geometry changes fastest — the sweep/taper breaks and, above all, the control-surface edges (a boxcar in control gain). The panel mesh is frozen at the panelling study's selection c16s2u; this study moves only the section placement and count.

The trap — and how the prior study fell into it

With no CFD, the reference is a dense AVL mesh. DECISION-0011 used a 49-section uniform reference — which shares a family with the uniform candidate and is biased toward it. Worse, requiring uniform / adaptive / clustered to all agree is not a legitimacy test at all: adaptive stays non-uniform by design, so it need not match a dense uniform mesh. The correct test is Richardson convergence of dense uniform in N — and that is what makes the reference here legitimate.

Stage 1A legitimate reference, and separability

Dense uniform is Richardson-converged: it changes < 0.6% (typically < 0.4%) between N=151 and N=201 on every quantity, so uniform-201 is legitimate ground truth. The section-placement effect is separable from the panel mesh — 2.74% at c16s2u vs 3.04% at the cheap c8s1u — so the reference is built where N≈201 is affordable (c16s2u caps at N≈92 under the vortex limit) and the placement policy transfers back.

Two quantities are placement-limited

Induced drag `cd_ind` stays ~2–3% placement-sensitive even at N=201: AVL's Trefftz integral depends on the spanwise strip distribution, which differs between placements irreducibly. `hinge_sym` sits near AVL's 6-decimal print floor. Both are reported separately and not held to the < 1% gate — the honest analogue of a noise floor.

Stage 1d — the money resultThe hard points do all the work

Ablate the mandatory nodes. Uniform placement without pins is catastrophic (21% worst-case); with the hard-point pins it drops to 5%. Adaptive density, layered on top of the same pins, is ~2× worse than pinned uniform.

The decisive testAdaptive loses on the hard cases too

Adaptive was meant to earn its keep on the difficult geometries — narrow bands, sharp sweep, high taper. Against the converged reference it loses 6/6. It does lower the control-gain ramp fraction (it does what it is told) — but that does not translate into accuracy; equidistribution starves loaded regions and the error rises.

Stage 3How many sections — the real lever

With pinned uniform, typical accuracy is excellent by N=25 and N≈49 gets 90% of cells under 1%. A few control-derivative cells on specific designs plateau at ~1.8–2.9% and do not improve with N — section-limited, echoing the panelling study's "control effectiveness under-resolved" theme.

What to do

  • DoE / optimisation: uniform placement, section_placement = never, keep snap_sections_to_control = true — the pins are the value. Adaptive density OFF.
  • Count: N=25 for screening (median 0.64%); raise toward ~49 when control-derivative accuracy must be tight. Do not expect <1% worst-case on cd_ind/hinge from any setting.
  • `ramp_fraction` stays a useful diagnostic; it must not drive placement. `concentration()` remains unused (it never predicted difficulty).
Honest scope (what still needs confirming)

Established on 14 designs × 3 angles at the validation control state, on the c8s1u reference with policy transfer justified by separability — robust (adaptive lost on all 14). Not yet run: the full 30-design DoE ranking gate, the trim/stability decision gate (|Δδ_trim| ≤ 0.5°), deflection transfer, and honest timings. The placement conclusion is monotone and unanimous and is unlikely to move; the count recommendation should be confirmed against the trim gate. The reference is converged-section AVL, not CFD — AVL's own ~1.5% model error sits underneath.
