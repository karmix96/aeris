Where the wing sections go

Result

Place sections uniformly and pin the hard points. Do not adapt the density.

At the production mesh, across all 36 designs, the entire benefit comes from pinning the mandatory nodes — control-surface band edges, planform breaks, fixed-airfoil stations — which cuts the median section error from 12.7% to 1.0% (worst-case 34.8%→7.8%), on 35 of 36 designs. Information-weighted adaptive density placement never helps — it beats pinned-uniform on 0/36 designs, and loses outright (14/14) against a fully converged reference. The accuracy lever is the section count, not the distribution.

This revises DECISION-0011: its "adaptive helps" result was the snapping (pinning) effect misattributed to the density rule, and was measured against a non-converged reference.

The problemSections, and the ground-truth trap

AVL interpolates the wing piecewise-linearly between defining spanwise sections. Placing them well should matter most where the geometry changes fastest — the sweep/taper breaks and, above all, the control-surface edges (a boxcar in control gain). The panel mesh is frozen at the panelling study's selection c16s2u; this study moves only the section placement and count.

The trap — and how the prior study fell into it

With no CFD, the reference is a dense AVL mesh. DECISION-0011 used a 49-section uniform reference — which shares a family with the uniform candidate and is biased toward it. Worse, requiring uniform / adaptive / clustered to all agree is not a legitimacy test at all: adaptive stays non-uniform by design, so it need not match a dense uniform mesh. The correct test is Richardson convergence of dense uniform in N — and that is what makes the reference here legitimate.

Stage 1A legitimate reference, and separability

Dense uniform is Richardson-converged: it changes < 0.6% (typically < 0.4%) between N=151 and N=201 on every quantity, so uniform-201 is legitimate ground truth. The section-placement effect is separable from the panel mesh — 2.74% at c16s2u vs 3.04% at the cheap c8s1u — so the reference is built where N≈201 is affordable (c16s2u caps at N≈92 under the vortex limit) and the placement policy transfers back.

Two quantities are placement-limited

Induced drag `cd_ind` stays ~2–3% placement-sensitive even at N=201: AVL's Trefftz integral depends on the spanwise strip distribution, which differs between placements irreducibly. `hinge_sym` sits near AVL's 6-decimal print floor. Both are reported separately and not held to the < 1% gate — the honest analogue of a noise floor.

Geometry, complexity & placementWhat the sections actually see

Before the result, the mechanism. AVL interpolates the wing piecewise-linearly between sections, so a spanwise "information density" ρ(y) ∝ |g″|½ (de Boor) marks where straight-line interpolation hurts most. Adaptive placement equidistributes that density; uniform ignores it. Both pin the hard points.

Design-set coverage

The maps below show two representative geometries (a span-map is inherently per-design), but every quantitative result is over the full set of 36 designs — 30 Latin-hypercube samples spanning the 19-variable DoE plus 6 hand-built extremes. The placement decision (§below) is all 36 at the production mesh; the complexity pattern (last figure of this section) is verified across all 36; the reference-legitimacy and count-convergence sub-studies use 8–14-design subsets because each design there needs 5–6 separate section counts.

Why the complexity index does not win

Equidistribution minimises the interpolation error of the monitored geometry functions — it clusters nodes where |g″| is large. But two things break the link to aerodynamic error: (i) the two dominant features it finds — the control-band edges and the planform breaks — are kinks the |g″|½ monitor cannot resolve anyway, and are already captured exactly by hard-point pins; and (ii) once those are pinned, the remaining "high-curvature" signal is weak, so the rule spends the free budget chasing minor wiggles and pulls nodes away from the smooth inboard span — which carries the largest chord and the most load. Minimising geometric curvature error is not minimising aerodynamic error when the load is not where the curvature is. A loading-weighted (goal-oriented) monitor could in principle fix this; the pure geometric one tested here cannot, and loses on every design.

The money resultThe hard points do all the work

Ablate the mandatory nodes — at the production mesh, all 36 designs. Uniform placement without pins is catastrophic (median 12.7%, worst 34.8%); with the hard-point pins it collapses to a 1.0% median. Adaptive density, on the same pins, is worse — it never beats pinned-uniform.

What the production mesh forced into the open

c16s2u cannot host a converged section reference — its 6000-vortex cap limits sections to N≈89, where dense uniform is converged only to ~1% (33/36 designs < 1%, three sensitive outliers up to 14%). A converged reference needs N≈150–200, affordable only at reduced panel density. So the placement verdict is decided here; the count question is genuinely undecidable at c16s2u and is settled on the separability-justified reference mesh below.

Converged-reference cross-checkAdaptive loses on the hard cases too

The production-mesh reference is vortex-capped, so the adaptive-vs-uniform contrast is confirmed against the fully converged c8s1u reference (uniform-201). On the difficult geometries — narrow bands, sharp sweep, high taper — adaptive loses 6/6. It does lower the control-gain ramp fraction (it does what it is told) — but that does not translate into accuracy; equidistribution starves loaded regions and the error rises.

Stage 3How many sections — the real lever

With pinned uniform, typical accuracy is excellent by N=25 and N≈49 gets 90% of cells under 1%. A few control-derivative cells on specific designs plateau at ~1.8–2.9% and do not improve with N — section-limited, echoing the panelling study's "control effectiveness under-resolved" theme.

What to do

  • DoE / optimisation: uniform placement, section_placement = never, keep snap_sections_to_control = true — the pins are the value. Adaptive density OFF.
  • Count: N=25 for screening (median 0.64%); raise toward ~49 when control-derivative accuracy must be tight. Do not expect <1% worst-case on cd_ind/hinge from any setting.
  • `ramp_fraction` stays a useful diagnostic; it must not drive placement. `concentration()` remains unused (it never predicted difficulty).
Honest scope (what still needs confirming)

Established on 14 designs × 3 angles at the validation control state, on the c8s1u reference with policy transfer justified by separability — robust (adaptive lost on all 14). Not yet run: the full 30-design DoE ranking gate, the trim/stability decision gate (|Δδ_trim| ≤ 0.5°), deflection transfer, and honest timings. The placement conclusion is monotone and unanimous and is unlikely to move; the count recommendation should be confirmed against the trim gate. The reference is converged-section AVL, not CFD — AVL's own ~1.5% model error sits underneath.
